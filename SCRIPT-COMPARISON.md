Side-by-side of the current Scale Systems sales script (selling the AI Content System,
$25k / $5k founding price) against Cole Gordon's 6-phase funnel as captured in this database.

**Verdict:** the script is ~75% aligned with Cole's methodology and strong in places most
scripts are weak (objection *prevention*, temp checks). Six concrete gaps below, ranked by
impact.

## The 6 gaps, ranked

### 1. ❌ No post-price objection handling — BIGGEST GAP

After the price drop the script goes straight to "all I'm gonna need is your 16-digit card
number." If the prospect says *"it's too expensive" / "I need to think about it" / "I need
to talk to my partner"* — the rep is unarmed. The Obstacle Busting section is *prevention*
(good), but Cole's core teaching is: **expect 1-2 objections AFTER the price drop, then
close.**

> "Don't expect to get the close when you drop the investment. Expect to get the close
> after you drop the investment, you handle three objections, and then they buy."

**Fix:** add a post-price objection section. The universal handle:
> "No problem. Just so I can stay organized — [money/that] aside, is there anything else
> keeping you from being less than 100% certain this is what you need? ... So you're 100%
> in?" → then route: price → `objections/price-and-financial.md`, think-about-it →
> `objections/think-about-it.md`, spouse → `objections/support-spouse-partner-team.md`.

Handle **uncertainty before logistics, every time.** Full routing: `FUNNEL.md`.

### 2. ❌ No frame at the start of the call

The script goes rapport → "what do you want to have happen by end of this meeting." There's
no agenda-set. Cole: framing gives the rep control and is normal in any business meeting.

**Fix — insert after rapport, before the outcome question:**
> "Well [name], what I've really found to work best on these calls is first just diving
> deeper into the specifics of your situation — what's working, what's not, and what you
> feel are the biggest challenges keeping you from moving forward. Once we get clarity
> there, if we can help I'm happy to walk you through what that looks like — and if not,
> I'll point you in the right direction. Cool?"

### 3. ❌ No moment-of-decision question in discovery

The probing list is thorough but never asks for the *specific story* of when they decided
enough was enough. Cole calls this the single highest-leverage discovery question — "deep
enough is deep enough is finding the moment of decision," because reliving it resurfaces
the emotions of change.

**Fix — add to the probing questions:**
> "Take me back to the moment you realised this had to change — what actually happened?
> What was going on that day?"

### 4. ❌ Pitch has no high-level promise

The pitch opens straight into the Japan analogy (pillar 1). Cole's pitch codex opens with
the **high-level promise** — frame the whole thing before the pillars.

**Fix — say this right before the Japan story:**
> "Let me preface this by saying everything we do is customized. So for you specifically,
> it's gonna be three things — three things to take you from [current: posting with no
> revenue] to [desired: their stated goal] while [removing their stated pain]. Here's the
> first…"

### 5. ❌ No hamburger transition

The script transitions itself ("before I break down HOW we can help…"). Cole's transition
gets the *prospect* to ask to be pitched = zero sales resistance.

**Fix — after "is there anything else I need to know":**
> "So where do you think we should go from here? I can pretty much walk you through exactly
> how we'd help you get to [outcome] if you want — but you tell me where you want to go."
> *(equal/downward inflection — never upward)*

### 6. ⚠️ The close is abrupt

"All I'm gonna need is your 16-digit card number" jumps straight to the card. Cole's
assumptive close ladders in softer — billing address is easier to say than a card number.

**Fix:**
> "How do you feel? ... Cool, you sure? ... You ready to get started on some homework? ...
> What's your billing address?" → then the card.

## What the script already does WELL — do NOT change these

- **Obstacle Busting** — pre-handling partner, self-doubt, doubt-in-us, money, platform
  risk *in discovery*. This is objection prevention done right; most scripts skip it
  entirely. Cole's whole philosophy is "eliminate objections before the close." ✅
- **Problems-first vs. goals-first swap** — matches Cole's two discovery syntaxes exactly.
- **Permission-reason-why** in "Label The Goal" ("can I ask a personal question, the reason
  I'm asking is…") — textbook Cole.
- **"Biggest chaaaallenge… or what's not working at the level it truly could be"** — almost
  word-for-word Cole's first discovery question.
- **The soft temp check + alignment line** ("what's important to us is alignment, our team
  is all in") — near-verbatim Cole. ✅✅
- **Scale of 1-10** temp check + the double tie-down ("anything keeping you less than 100%
  certain this is the right thing and now's the right time"). ✅
- **"It's not your fault"** pre-pitch + onboarding-before-price + the case study in the
  pitch + the trade on payment plans ("if that's something I can do, is that something
  you'd move forward with here now?"). All ✅.

## Two smaller refinements

- **Past Pain** is thin ("what did you like or dislike about it"). Strengthen with Cole's
  duplicate question: *"What we've found, doing this with thousands of clients, is it comes
  down to one of two things — either [X] or [Y]. Which was it for you?"* — it makes them
  self-diagnose in a way you can then handle.
- **Payment plan:** the script asks "what would you need?" — Cole would run the open-wallet
  first (net 30-day cash → cash on hand → credit) to find the biggest plan they can
  *ethically* do, rather than letting the prospect set the terms. See
  `objections/price-and-financial.md`.
