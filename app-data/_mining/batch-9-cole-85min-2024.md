## The committing phase framework (Cole verbatim)

> "The committing phase has three parts. **Questions, small temp, what's
> next?** Objections has three parts. **Price drop, pacing the first
> objection, and then isolate and handle with logistics.**"

## What Cole calls the closing process — verbatim

> "I call that process **pacing the first objection** where you get the
> **tie down, the double tie down, then you isolate**. They need a process.
> When they don't have a process and then they're triggered and they're in
> fight or flight, they do shit like this [giving payment plans as objection
> handling]. It's desperate and pathetic."

## The 5-step Universal Handle (Cole's actual order from this transcript)

1. **Diffuse** — "No problem." Lower the temperature.
2. **Tie down (the 100% question)** — "Before we get into any of that — how
   do you feel? You mentioned you want to scale your business to [outcome].
   Do you feel like this is 100% what you need to be able to get there?"
3. **Double tie down** — "Great. So what you're telling me is — [X] aside —
   you're 100% in?"
4. **Isolate** — "Outside of that, is there anything else keeping you from
   being less than 100% certain this is what you need?"
5. **Handle + re-tie down** — Address the specific logistic (financial /
   support / timing). Trade for decision: "If I solved [X] for you, are
   you 100% moving forward right now?"

## On the tonality of the tie-down (CRITICAL — Cole verbatim)

> "It can't be soft. It can't be passive. It has to be very direct. Because
> what I'm going for here, and this is a little counterintuitive, I'm kind
> of going for like a no. I really want to suss out any uncertainty that
> they might be having on this call so I can address it so we can have an
> honest conversation."

> "I purposely use the language **100%** — very definitive language — and
> I'm very direct when I say it. … You must **command honesty**. That's
> not coming from this like 'I command honesty' tone. It's more of like a
> genuine thing — a vibe that we're just not going to bullshit each other
> on this call."

## Three objection types (Cole's taxonomy)

- **Uncertainty** — anything keeping them less than 100% certain.
  "I need to think about it / next quarter / research / read reviews /
  talk to your clients / case studies". HANDLE FIRST, ALWAYS.
- **Support (logistic)** — they're 100% in but have to check with someone
  else. Spouse / business partner / board / team. NOT a smoke screen.
- **Financial (logistic)** — they're 100% in but physically need to move
  money around or break up the payment.

> "Uncertainties [are] true objections. Support and financials [are]
> logistics. We have to handle the uncertainty first. Then we can move
> on to the logistics."

## Example 1 — "I need to think about it / send me the info" (sleep-on-it)

Cole's exact verbatim flow:

1. Prospect: "This sounds great. I need to think about it. Can you send
   this over to me so I can think about it and get back to you next week?"
2. Rep (diffuse + tie down): "**No problem. Before we get into any of
   that — how do you feel? You mentioned you want to scale your business
   to 100 grand a month. Do you feel like this is 100% what you need to
   be able to get there and this is 100% going to work?**"
3. Prospect: "No, I do — I really like this. I just don't make decisions
   on the first call. It's kind of my decision-making process."
4. Rep (isolate sleep-on-it): "**No issue at all. I'm totally good with
   you sleeping on it. And honestly, based on your experience in the past,
   I would sleep on it too if I was you. So just to clarify — if we
   schedule a time tomorrow and you wake up feeling the exact same way
   you feel right now, then what you're telling me is we're 100%
   processing the payment and moving forward.**"

## Example 2 — financial logistic

1. Same opener / tie-down.
2. Prospect: "No, I definitely want to do this. It's just a matter of how
   I'm going to come up with the money."
3. Rep (double tie-down): "**Great. So what you're telling me is —
   money aside — you're 100% in?**"
4. Prospect: "Yes."
5. Rep (permission for open wallet): "**So look, most of our clients do
   it up front. But for certain clients, depending on the situation, we
   break it up. So are you open to having an honest conversation
   financially, getting everything out on the table, so we can see if
   there's a way we can make this work now or at the very least we can
   create a game plan together to make this work in the future?**"
6. Open wallet sequence: 30-day net incoming → cash on hand exactly →
   credit access.
7. Re-anchor: "**And you really want to do this, right?**"
8. Trade concession for decision: "**I don't think the best thing for
   you right now is to do this all up front… I also don't think the best
   thing is to do nothing. So what I'd be willing to do for you is split
   it into two — half now, half in 30 days. If I'm willing to do that
   for you, will you move forward right now?**"

## Example 3 — spouse/partner

1. Same opener.
2. Tie-down: "**No problem. Just so I could be clear — how do you feel
   for you personally? Do you 100% want to do this?**"
3. Double tie-down: "**Cool. Just to clarify — if it was solely your
   decision, we'd be processing the payment and you'd be 100% moving
   forward.**"
4. Respect vs collaboration probe: "**Is this something to where you
   basically made the decision and you're just going to let them know,
   or is this something you guys need to really collaborate on?**"
   Rule of thumb: anything but "I'm definitely doing this" = collaboration.

## Example 4 — been burned / solution-based uncertainty

1. Same opener / tie-down.
2. Prospect: "Yeah, I like everything you said on paper, but you never
   know until you actually do it. We tried somebody else in the past
   that was similar and it didn't work."
3. Rep (isolate the "burned" hesitation from the solution): "**Totally
   get it. And yeah, that's super frustrating. Like, a lot of times
   we're put in the place where we have to pick up the pieces of
   somebody overpromising and then deliver. So just so I can be clear —
   is there anything about what I outlined specifically that's keeping
   you from being less than 100% certain that this is going to work?
   Or is it purely a hesitation based on that bad experience that's
   just really giving you pause?**"
4. Apples-to-oranges comparison move when ready: ask questions about the
   prior provider (clients, revenue, sales team, case studies, time in
   market) → then compare to current offering with direct certainty.

## Pitch-stage rules referenced in this video

- **Clarity and certainty** — pitch must be the simultaneous explanation
  of why their past attempts failed AND why this is going to be different.
  "Russell Brunson → ClickFunnels: WordPress sites were the status quo,
  ClickFunnels was the simultaneous explanation of why websites were
  just business cards and why funnels were the new superior way."
- "**If they're confused, they can't buy. Like this is fundamentals 101.**"

## Committing-phase questions framework (Cole verbatim)

When prospect asks a question:
1. **Clarify the question before answering** — "Just so I know and can
   answer your question, is there a specific reason why you're asking
   that?"
2. **Answer briefly, specifically** — 2 sentences or less.
3. **End with "Great question. What else? What are the questions?"** —
   keeps you in leadership of the call.

## Follow-up framing (counterintuitive)

> "**Don't feel like you have to come to the next call with any sort of
> decision. All I want you to do is come to the call with questions… let's
> cover the questions along with the information that I have and then
> once you have all the information, we could figure out whatever
> decision for you is the best decision.**"

Reason (verbatim):
> "By default, if you do not give them any other reasons to show up to
> the follow-up call, in their brain the only other reason to show up
> is to tell you yes. So if how they feel that day when your follow-up
> is set is no or more likely even **not yet**, they will just not show
> up because why would they?"

## Key tonality principle (closing)

> "It's a vibe — we are just not going to bullshit each other. I'm not
> going to bullshit you. I'm not going to do any overselling. But I
> want you to be honest with me."
