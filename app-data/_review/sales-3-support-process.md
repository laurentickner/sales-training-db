# Audit — SUPPORT & PROCESS bucket objections

Scope: `app-data/objection-responses.json` entries `spouse-partner`, `multiple-partners`,
`discuss-with-team`, `prospect-taking-control`. Cross-checked against
`_mining/batch-1..8.md` and `OBJECTION-PLAYBOOK.md`.

Overall: the four entries are faithful to Cole's method in skeleton and survive the audit
better than most. No fabricated mechanics. Issues below are mostly **missing steps and
out-of-order sequencing**, not invented content.

---

## WRONG / RISKY

### 1. `spouse-partner` — the universal pre-isolation steps are SKIPPED (sequence error)

Cole's iron rule (stated in this app's own `universal_framework` and `OBJECTION-PLAYBOOK`
§"universal handle") is: **DIFFUSE → ISOLATE (should/how) → TEMP CHECK → SCALE → DOUBLE
TIE-DOWN → handle.** Support objections run through it like any other.

The app's `spouse-partner` step 1 jumps straight to:
> "No problem. Just so I can be clear — how do you feel for you personally? Do you 100% want to do this?"

It collapses the temp check into one short line and **omits the process/"is it 100% going
to work" half** that Cole always asks. Verbatim source (batch-2 line 18):
> "no problem now we'll circle back to that in just a second and i have no issue with that
> but how do you feel about the process like we covered like four key steps and four key
> pillars ... do you really feel like this is what you need to be able to get to that goal"

The risk is real: support objections are LOGISTICS, so the universal rule says clear
UNCERTAINTY first. The app's step 1 only half-tests certainty ("do you want this") and
never tests belief ("is it 100% going to work"). A prospect who is secretly unsure can
sail through and the rep chases a spouse who was never the real blocker. The entry's own
`do_not` line says exactly this — but the steps don't enforce it.

**Fix:** Step 1 should be the full temp check on BOTH personal desire AND belief the
process works, e.g. *"talking to your spouse aside — do you feel this is 100% what you
need to get to [goal], and that it's 100% going to work?"* — then the "if it was solely
your decision" tie-down.

### 2. `spouse-partner` step 2 — "if it was solely your decision" misframes Cole's hypothetical

App step 2:
> "Cool. So just to clarify — if it was solely your decision, we'd be processing the payment
> and you'd be 100% moving forward right now?"

Cole never frames it as "if it was solely your decision." He frames it as **the spouse
already having given the green light** — a hypothetical where the partner is supportive,
not removed. Verbatim (batch-7 line 68, batch-8 line 84):
> "if your spouse is on this call and they're like, 'Hey, Bob, I believe you and do whatever
> is best for your business' ... If they had already given you that permission, would you
> be 100% moving forward and processing payment with me on this call right now?"

"If it was solely your decision" subtly tells the prospect their spouse's opinion *doesn't
count* — combative, and it contradicts the later two-conversations coaching, which is
built on respecting that the spouse's buy-in matters. Cole's version keeps the spouse in
the room and supportive. **Fix:** rewrite step 2 to the "if your spouse said do whatever's
best" hypothetical.

## SHOULD IMPROVE

### 3. `spouse-partner` — permission-vs-respect diagnostic is under-worded

App step 4:
> "is this a respect thing where you've basically decided and you're just letting them know,
> or do you genuinely need to sit down, collaborate, and get their permission to move forward?"

Close, but Cole's verbatim is sharper and the app drops the key phrase "**full buy-in**"
and the "regardless" framing. Verbatim (batch-8 line 85):
> "is this like a respect thing to where you're going to do it regardless you're just going
> to let them know so you're 100 in you just got to let them know ... or two do you really
> need to have like a sit-down conversation run everything by them and get their permission
> and full buy-in to move forward"

Also worth surfacing the diagnostic question from batch-8 line 88 when the rep can't place
the spouse: *"Help me understand what kind of [thing] you're looking for in her approval
... if she's not a business partner, she's not a consultant, I'm just trying to better
understand where she fits in."* — currently missing entirely.

### 4. `spouse-partner` DISCOVERY — missing "are you on the same page about the PROBLEM"

App step 5 (DISCOVERY) lists: what will they think / what will they say re investment /
are they supportive / do they know you're on this call. Good, but it omits Cole's
**problem-alignment** question, which is the one that does the real work. Verbatim
(batch-2 line 13):
> "was she supportive of you trying to build your business in a way in which it's
> predictable ... are you guys on the same page about that problem"

And the financial-decision-rule probe (batch-2 line 14):
> "based on where you guys are at financially right now really any big decision is
> something that you guys need to sit down and talk about correct"

These two convert the objection from "I need her permission" to "we already agree the
problem must be fixed" — add them to the discovery step.

### 5. `spouse-partner` — two-conversations coaching is solid but missing the closing line

The TWO CONVERSATIONS COACHING step is faithful (matches batch-1 line 56, batch-2 line 15,
batch-7 line 75). The app even keeps the payoff: *"if you're not certain, neither will
they be, nor should they be."* Good.

But it drops Cole's follow-on — the **certainty-check after the coaching** (batch-2 line 17):
> "are you sure because if there's anything else that's keeping you from being less than 100
> certain that you're going to be able to show up that way then let's have a conversation
> about that now and get on the same side of the table with me"

The two-conversations bit only works if the prospect is genuinely certain — Cole re-tests
right after delivering it. Add that re-tie-down before the middle-ground close.

### 6. `spouse-partner` MIDDLE-GROUND CLOSE — pre-qualifier and refund language watered down

App's middle-ground close is structurally right (deposit + onboard + call with spouse +
refund + concession trade). But two things from the verbatim are missing:

(a) Cole **pre-qualifies certainty before presenting the deposit** — batch-8 line 90:
> "Are you open to a middle ground solution? ... Before I present it, shoot it with me
> straight. Do you actually want to do this ... You're 10 out of 10?"

The app jumps straight into "Let's put down a refundable deposit now" with no
"are you open to a middle ground / are you a 10" gate. Add it.

(b) The app says generic "If they hate it, we refund it." Cole's verbatim is more vivid
and lower-pressure — batch-8 line 91 / batch-2 line 29: *"if she actually hates our guts
and thinks the onboarding is the worst thing ever ... we'll just send it back"* /
*"we're not in the business of antique marriages."* Minor, but the lighter tone is
deliberate de-pressuring. Optional polish.

### 7. `multiple-partners` — person-by-person temp check loses the "out of 10" verbatim

App step 1:
> "Go person by person on the temp check: 'Jim, where are you out of 10? ... Mark, where
> are you out of 10? ... Alex, looks like you're the only one not certain — it's a
> two-to-three vote, looks like we're good to go.'"

This is faithful (batch-7 line 83) and the "two-to-three vote" close is preserved. Good.
One nuance lost: Cole opens each with "**just so I can understand**" — the same
stay-organized framing he uses everywhere — which keeps it from feeling like a poll/
ambush. Minor wording add.

Bigger gap: the entry has no DIFFUSE/ISOLATE/temp-check-on-the-primary-contact opening at
all — it starts cold with "go person by person." In Cole's flow you still run the
universal handle on the person on the call first. Should at least cross-reference the
universal framework.

### 8. `prospect-taking-control` — customized-frame redirect is faithful; add the energy/puzzle nuance

The three steps map cleanly to batch-5 lines 62/63/65 ("When Prospects Try To Take CONTROL
Of The Call"). The customized-context redirect, the "I misheard / that's my bad" take-
responsibility move, and match-their-energy are all present and verbatim-accurate. This is
the strongest of the four entries — no correction needed. Only optional add: Cole's
"treat it as a fun puzzle, not a threat" is in the app already; the verbatim also stresses
this is "**the most time-efficient way**" as the explicit justification — the app keeps
that. Fine as-is.

## GAPS / MISSING

### 9. `discuss-with-team` — missing the "is this the #1 constraint" setup and the bandwidth verbatim

App step 1 opens: *"If you believe this is the number one constraint in your business, your
job as the CEO is to determine the problem, the priority, the vision..."* — faithful to
batch-7 line 91 / batch-1 line 61. The downward-authority "buy-in not permission" line is
present and correct. Good.

What's missing: the **genuine-bandwidth pre-handle wording**. The app step 3 says "get the
implementer on a call, or pre-handle it" but gives only a paraphrase. The corpus treats
team-bandwidth as a distinct sub-case (batch-5 line 45 lists *"i don't know if my team is
going to have the bandwidth"* and *"compliance team"* / *"board"* as triggers; batch-7
line 87 lists *"my team is going to implement this"*). The entry's triggers do include
these, but there's no verbatim pre-frame question to surface bandwidth during discovery.
Add a concrete line, e.g. *"does your team have the bandwidth to implement this this
quarter?"* asked BEFORE the pitch — the app currently only mentions it reactively.

### 10. `discuss-with-team` — no temp-check / certainty gate before the CEO reframe

The CEO/buy-in reframe is a confrontational move. Cole only deploys it once the prospect
themselves is sold — same iron rule as spouse. The entry has no opening certainty check
and no `do_not` line. It should carry the same guard as `spouse-partner`:
**never run the downward-authority reframe before the prospect is 100% sold.** Currently
missing — add a `do_not`.

### 11. `multiple-partners` — missing the "your partners weren't on the call" empathy bridge wording

App step 2 paraphrases it ("Your partners weren't on this call, so they won't have all the
information, and it's a lot for you to relay"). The verbatim (batch-7 line 84) is fuller
and includes the **"I don't want to take up a full hour of their time"** courtesy framing
and "**all four of us can make a decision together**" — the collaborative close. The app's
version is adequate but trims the relationship-preserving language that makes the recap
call feel like a favor, not a stall. Minor.

### 12. `spouse-partner` — no follow-up / "come with questions" fallback

When the deposit close fails and a genuine spouse conversation must happen, Cole has a
specific low-pressure follow-up frame (batch-1 line 226, batch-2 line 32):
> "go talk to her and then let's [set] up another call ... grab any questions ... if you
> still feel good we can definitely talk about getting started ... regardless let's keep
> the communication open"

plus the definitive tie-down for the follow-up call. The `multiple-partners` entry HAS a
tie-down step ("when we talk tomorrow ... we're 100% moving forward, yes or no?"); the
`spouse-partner` entry has **no follow-up tie-down at all** — it ends on the middle-ground
close. If the deposit is declined the rep is left with nothing. Add a final fallback step
mirroring `multiple-partners` step 3: lock a next-day call with a definitive tie-down.

### 13. `spouse-partner` — discovery prevention step (qualify support in discovery) not referenced

Cole's strongest spouse handling is **prevention** — he qualifies the partner during
discovery so it never becomes an objection. Verbatim (batch-3 line 63, batch-4 line 144):
> "are you married, do you have a family ... is your partner supportive of you trying to
> [solve X] ... do they know you're on this call? what would they think if they knew?"

This is a sales-process step, not strictly an objection rebuttal, so its absence from the
objection entry is defensible — but a one-line cross-reference ("ideally pre-qualified in
discovery — see sales-process") would help the rep understand this objection should rarely
reach the call's close.
