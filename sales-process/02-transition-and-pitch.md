# Phase 3-4: Transition & Pitch (The Pitch Codex)

Verbatim scripting for moving from discovery into the pitch, and the pitch itself. Full
unabridged versions in `transcripts/raw/` (`cole-pitch-codex-sell-anything.md`,
`cole-3.2m-pitch-packaging.md`, `cole-closed-4126-clients-pt2.md`).

## PHASE 3 — The Transition

Goal: get from question-asking to pitching with **zero sales resistance** — by getting the
prospect to *ask you* to pitch them. Never pitch without permission.

### The basic transition

> "Look, I don't think I have any more questions — is there anything else you feel like we
> haven't covered that you feel like I need to know?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Pt2]
- **Why:** Replaces the contrived "let me recap your situation." They answer "no, I feel
  like you totally get where I'm at" — you get the recap in one sentence, in *their* words.

Then:

> "Well, John, based on what you told me previously, we can **definitely help**." [pause]

- **Note:** Say "definitely help" — not "I think there may be a possibility we could help."
  Trust-based hedging "sounds extremely pathetic" on reviewed calls. — [Cole-Pt2]

Optionally insert a client story / personal story / valuable insight on the *what* (not the
*how*) to build tension and tee up the pitch.

### The hamburger transition (question → suggestion → question)

> "So where do you think we should go from here? I can pretty much walk you through exactly
> how we'd help you get to [outcome] if you want — but you tell me where you want to go."

- **Source:** [Cole-Pt2]
- **Why:** Question-suggestion-question. The suggestion in the middle is what you want them
  to pick; the questions give the illusion of control. They always take the suggestion and
  ask you to pitch them. **Must use equal or downward inflection** — upward inflection makes
  it "sound like a pathetic teenager."

## PHASE 4 — The Pitch (The Pitch Codex)

Three parts: **high-level promise → bridge → delivery.** Most of the time is on the promise
and the bridge; delivery (the actual deliverables) is the *shortest* part, ~60 seconds.

**The non-negotiable principle:** the bridge must be the **simultaneous explanation of why
everything they tried in the past has failed and why this is going to be different.**

### Set up the pen and pad (direct command)

> "Do you have a pen and pad? ... On your pen and pad, write out one through four. One,
> two, three, four." [pause — wait until they're ready]

- **Source:** [Cole-PitchCodex]
- **Note:** "Write out one through four" — direct command, not "you can if you want to."
  Re-assesses the buying situation and gets them internalizing.

### High-level promise — verbatim

> "Cool — well, let me preface this by saying everything we do is all customized. So for
> you specifically, it's gonna be four things. Four things to help you go from [current
> situation] to [desired situation] while [removing pain / getting benefit]."

- **Source:** [Cole-PitchCodex]

Optionally open with "what it's NOT" to break preconceived objections:

> "What this is not going to be is a group coaching program with a bunch of modules and two
> calls a week with 50 other people. What this is going to be is a step-by-step system where
> we roll up the sleeves, get in the trenches with you, and build out [the system]."

- **Source:** [Cole-Packaging]

### The bridge — 4 pillars

Pillars 1 & 2 = **paradigm shifts** (why they failed, why this is different — the sale
hinges here). Pillars 3 & 4 = **future pacing** (what you do *after* the main result —
builds certainty + eliminates fear of success).

**Pillar formula** — verbatim skeleton:

> "When it comes to [desired outcome], here's the problem: most people out there are doing
> [incorrect behavior], and because of that they end up having [problem], which ultimately
> means [consequence]. So instead, what we do is [your method / feature], which allows you
> to [benefit], and ultimately means [benefit of the benefit]. Does that make sense? Cool —
> what are your thoughts on that?"

- **Source:** [Cole-PitchCodex]
- **Mechanics:** us-vs-them frame + problem-linking + benefit-linking. Advanced version
  prepends: problem → reason why → cost → feature → benefit → benefit-of-the-benefit.

Worked example (verbatim):

> "The very first thing we're gonna do is help you build out your productized offer. When
> it comes to dialing in your paid traffic, here's the issue — most coaches out there are
> completely focused on their ads, their funnels, their CPCs, their bidding strategies. So
> they spend a ton of money on ads testing a bunch of stuff, when in reality it's their
> offer — it's like they're building a house on a foundation of sand. So for example, you
> know how earlier you said competitor XYZ is crushing it on ads and you can barely break
> even? Well, why is that — you're both offering the same thing? It's because of the offer.
> That's the exact reason we were able to do a million-plus just off 150k ad spend. So
> instead, what we do first is take you through an offer intensive to build out an offer
> that's unique, different, superior — which not only makes your ads work, but lets you
> command premium pricing, which makes the economics work. Does that make sense? Cool, what
> are your thoughts on that?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Pt2] / [Cole-PitchCodex]

### Pitch rules

- **Dialogue, not an anvil drop.** Never "save your questions for the end" then speed
  through. Space it out: "does that make sense? cool, what are your thoughts on that?"
- **Double tie-down** after each big pillar — "does that make sense?" (always yes) then
  "what are your thoughts on that?" (gets a real response). Optional third tie-down: "do
  you feel like, based on what we talked about earlier with [blank], this will help you?
  ... why though?"
- **Incorporate discovery** — their words beat yours: "earlier you told me [blank], and it
  was making you experience [blank] — well, that's the exact reason what we do is..."
- **Multiple forms of proof** per claim (aim for 3): reason-why proof (third-grade-language
  logic), living proof (your story), social proof (case study / quote), analogy/metaphor.
- **Name pillars with common terms that sound new in context** — "productized offer,"
  "finding and filtering," "ketosis." Never "the XYZ black-box system."

### Delivery (~60 seconds, the shortest part)

> "So you're going to be assigned an account manager, you'll have access pretty much
> 24/7/365, one-on-one calls every two weeks, Voxer support, the group, and the course
> material. And the reason we do it that way is so that instead of [blank] you can actually
> [benefit] so that you can [benefit of the benefit]."

- **Source:** [Cole-Packaging]
- End delivery on a series of benefits, then go straight into the temp check (committing
  phase — see file 03).
