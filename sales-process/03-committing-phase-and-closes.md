# Phase 5: Committing Phase, Price Drop & Closes

and closing. Full unabridged versions in `transcripts/raw/`

## The committing phase — 3 parts: temp check → onboarding → investment

Goal: get them 100% bought in on the **thesis** (the method) *before* money is on the table.

### Open it up for questions

> "So that's number four. What questions do you have about those four things specifically —
> about the process specifically?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Committing]
- **Note:** Say "about the process specifically" — not just "what questions do you have"
  (which invites "what's the price?"). Directs attention to the thesis.

### Answering their questions — 3 rules

1. **Check for a hidden objection** before answering. Classic example — "is there group
   coaching?":

> "Yeah, there's definitely parts of our program that are collaborative — I'll give you
> details on that, but just so I can answer correctly, is there a specific reason why
> you're asking?"

2. **Answer in as few words as possible** — one word if you can ("Absolutely." "100%."),
   two sentences max.
3. **Keep the lead** — after answering: "Does that make sense? Cool — what else, what are
   the questions?" (Don't go silent and let them take the call.)

- **Source:** [Cole-Committing] / [Cole-Pt3]

### The temp check

> "Cool — how do you feel? How do you feel about those four things we covered specifically?
> Do you feel like that's what you need to be able to get your [outcome]?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Committing]
- Listen for **tonality**. A real yes = fast, certain, "dude, absolutely." "I think so" =
  a no.

### The scale of 1 to 10 (when it's not an ecstatic yes)

> "I hear you. And just to be totally clear — what's really important to us is alignment.
> When you come and work with us, we're rolling up the sleeves, getting in the trenches
> with you, our team is all in. So it's important that you feel good about the process. So
> just to be 100% clear — on a scale of one to ten, one being 'I hate this guy, get me off
> the phone' and ten being 'that's exactly what I need' — where do you feel like you fall
> out exactly?"

> "Gotcha — I appreciate you being honest about that. What exactly do you think is keeping
> you from being an eight, nine, or a ten?"

- **Source:** [Cole-4Steps] / [Cole-Pt3]
- The 9-out-of-10 reframe: "Nothing in life is a 10 out of 10 — it's not even 100% certain
  you'll wake up tomorrow. So a 9 is about as close to 100% certain as you'll ever be."

### Onboarding before the price (paint the next cliff)

Always give the next steps BEFORE the number — so they're not leaping into a black abyss:

> "So you'll process the investment with me, and once we take care of that, what we do is
> set a baseline — we give you some homework right away, then we set up your implementation
> pathway call, where we decide when and how often we meet, what you do right away, what we
> do for you, the resources you need. So that call sets the stage for the next 30-60 days.
> Does that make sense? Cool — and the investment is just 10k."

- **Source:** [Cole-Committing] / [Cole-Pt3]

### The price drop + close

> "And the investment is just 10k." [then silent — but don't make it a weird standoff]

If 7-10 seconds pass: "How do you feel?" Then the yes-ladder close:

> "Cool — you sure? ... You ready to get started on some homework? ... What's your billing
> address?"

- **Source:** [Cole-4Steps]
- **Tonality:** soft, certain, downward inflection — "next steps are very simple, it's
  9,800." Don't pucker up and go rigid; that creates the awkward silence. The gentle
  downward-inflected period invites them to speak next.

## Closes gallery

### The deposit / refundable-deposit close — ALWAYS give a reason

> "Closers suck at deposits because they don't give a reason. If you give a reason you have
> ~90% success; if you don't, ~20%." — [Cole-Webinar]

### The 24-hour-refund "come in now" close

> "I don't think the best thing for you is to do nothing. So come in now — and if tomorrow
> you wake up and in the core of your bones you feel you made a terrible decision, 100% I'll
> just give your money back. But you just told me the reason you want to do this is to make
> an informed decision — and the only informed decision you can make is from actually being
> inside and seeing the product. So let's get you going today. Is that fair?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Webinar]

### The give-a-month close (timing objection)

> "I don't think the best thing for you is to lose two weeks of the program — but I also
> don't think the best thing is to do nothing. So what I'm willing to do is add 30 days
> onto your engagement. You start now, knock out the onboarding, and when your official
> start date comes around we hit the ground running. So if I was willing to do that for
> you, would you be willing to move forward right now?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Webinar] (full version in `objections/timing-start-later.md`)

### The payment-plan judo (the open wallet)

Never offer payment plans up front — use them as negotiating leverage at the close. Full
script: `objections/price-and-financial.md`. Skeleton: confirm "money aside you're 100%
in" → get permission → open wallet (net 30-day cash → cash on hand exactly → credit) →
"and you really want to do this, right?" → "I don't think the best thing is X, I also
don't think the best thing is nothing, so what I'd be willing to do is [half/third] down
— if I'm willing to do that for you, are you willing to move forward right now?" → trade
every concession for a decision (and a case study if you go to a 3-pay).

### The assumptive close

> "You ready to get started on some homework? Yep — what's your billing address?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Pt3] — "the one assumptive close that I think is pretty good."

### Nerves / monkey-brain closes

See `objections/nerves-cant-pull-trigger.md` — the nervousness close ("nerves are good,
nothing great was built by someone who wasn't a little nervous") and Brian's monkey-brain
close ("two parts of your brain... Bitcoin in 2010").

## Button down the sale

After the close: clearly restate next steps, reassure they made the right decision, and
mitigate buyer's remorse / refunds / cancellations. — [Cole-6Phases]

## Should you drop price early or late?

Cole's default: **late** (at the end of the committing phase) — especially for low-
sophistication or low-money markets that get shocked by the number. Early can work for
sophisticated B2B buyers deciding on value not price. Either way: **never** reveal payment
plans or the offer structure up front — they're closing leverage. — [Cole-Pt3]
