## PHASE 1 — Introduction (rapport + frame)

**Three outcomes of the intro:** (1) demonstrate you're a normal human, not a telemarketer;
(2) qualify they're in a good time/place to buy; (3) set the agenda. Keep rapport under
30 seconds — one or two volleys, not weather-talk.

### Rapport — verbatim

> "Hey, is this John? Hey John, it's just Cole here — what's up? ... Awesome man, I
> actually just got done with a workout, feeling really good, got the endorphins pumping,
> ready to talk to you. You been having a good week? Good week so far?"

- **Source:** [Cole-7Step]

### Qualify the buying situation + take the lead

> "Great, well I know we've got a limited amount of time here — so are you ready to jump
> in? You got a clean sheet of paper, something to take notes with?"

- **Source:** [Cole-7Step] / [Cole-6Phases]
- **Why:** On an audio call this sits them down, reveals if they're driving / at a kid's
  game, and gets them focused. If they're not in a buying situation, you can reposition.

### If they're skeptical / forgot the call (lean out to pull them in)

> "Oh, okay — gotcha. Well, is now still a good time to connect?"

- **Source:** [Cole-7Step]

### Frame the call — verbatim

> "Gotcha. Well, John, what I've really found to work best on these calls is first just
> diving deeper into the specifics of your business and your sales process — so what's
> working right now, what's not working right now, and ultimately what you really feel like
> are the biggest challenges that are keeping you from moving forward. And once we get some
> clarity there, if we can help, I'm more than happy to walk you through what that is — or
> if not, I can refer you to somebody I know, or just give you some homework to work on in
> the meantime. Cool?"

- **Source:** [Cole-7Step]

Sophisticated-market variant:

> "Yeah John, so I'm more than prepared to share with you some of the lead-generation
> strategies we use with real estate agents in your area — but everything we do is
> customized, so at this stage what probably makes the most sense is, let me get an idea of
> your lead gen right now, your market, the type of customers you want, and then based on
> that I can share with you the parts of what we do that'd be most relevant to you."

- **Source:** [Cole-7Step]

The first discovery question:

> "So that being said, probably our best place to start is — what would you say is, I
> guess, your biggest challenge in your business right now? Or like, what's not working at
> the level it truly could be, or that it should?"

- **Source:** [Cole-7Step]
- **Tonality note:** Stumble slightly, search for the words — it softens a direct question
  and reads as genuine. "What's not working at the level it could be" is a permission-giving
  softener. If the question doesn't fit your offer: "What stuck out to you about the ad that
  ultimately prompted you to reach out?"

## PHASE 2 — Information Gathering (discovery)

**Two goals:** understand IF and HOW you can help, and **eliminate objections before the
close**. Great closers don't get objections — they prevent them. Two question orders
("syntaxes"):

- **Problems-first syntax** — for **pain** (back pain, obesity, "no leads, business
  roller-coaster").
- **Goals-first syntax** — for **unfulfilled desires** (10% body fat → 7%, $3M → $10M,
  leaving a 9-5).

### The 8 question types

Background · Pain · Doubt · Financial/resource · Solution · Cost · Desire · Support.

### Goals-first syntax flow

**1. Isolate the challenge / why they're here**, then probe:

> "Tell me more. ... When you said [blank], what do you mean exactly? ... How do you mean
> exactly?" (Or repeat their last three words back as a question.)

**2. Desired situation** — ultimate goal → monetary goal → long-term vision → non-monetary
goal:

> "Gotcha — well, what might make more sense is to begin with the end in mind here. So,
> ultimately, what's the goal?"

> "And what's your monetary goal? ... It seems like you've thought of that number before —
> can I ask, why that number?"

Biz-op income probe (non-invasive way to learn what they earn):

> "How much money would you have to make just to replace the amount of income that you're
> making full-time? ... And is replacing that income enough to allow you to leave — or how
> much would you have to bring in to walk in the door, hand in your two weeks, and be done
> with your nine-to-five?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Pt2] / [Cole-7Step]

Clarify by assumption (people correct an assumption more readily than answer an open Q):

> "So you're making 5k a month right now then, correct?"

The has-that-put-you-in-a-tough-position probe (from Jeremy Miner):

> "Can I ask you an honest question — 5k a month, you have a family, kids... does that put
> you in a tough position financially? ... And in what way though?"

**3. Current situation** — covers pain, doubt, trust, cost, support. The **two-truths**
(from Eli):

> "Now — what do you do for work full-time? ... Do you like it?"

(They like it or they don't — either way you tee it up.) If they don't like it:

> "What's the worst part about that? ... Take me back to the last day where you were at
> work and had this sudden realization that enough is enough — I'm gonna draw the line in
> the sand, step across it, burn the boats. Take me back to that time — what happened?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Pt2]
- **Why:** This finds the **moment of decision**. "Deep enough is deep enough to find the
  moment of decision" — because reliving it resurfaces the emotions of change. Real
  examples from the calls: "a shooter walked into the school and his wife was teaching" /
  "my daughter hopped on my lap and said 'Mommy, you feel squishy.'"

### Doubt + solution questions

> "What's been the biggest thing keeping you from getting this off the ground? What's in
> the way?"

> "Have you reached out to anybody to help with this in the past? Are you working with
> anybody now? Have you been considering other coaches? Have you done coaching before?"

The **duplicate/triplicate question** (forces them to self-diagnose your way):

> "Look, I've had thousands of these conversations, and oftentimes we're left picking up
> the pieces after somebody overpromised and underdelivered. What we've found is, when
> people go into a program like you described and don't get the result, it's typically one
> of two things — either [blank] or [blank]. Which one of those do you think it was?"

- **Source:** [Cole-Pt2]

### Support questions

> "Are you married? ... Is your partner supportive of the change? ... Do they know you're
> on this call? What would they think if they knew you were on this call?"

- **Note:** Just gather here — do NOT handle a spouse objection in discovery. Save it for
  the committing phase. Reframing in discovery drags the call back to the surface.

## Question frameworks (the "get the gold" patterns)

Full version: `transcripts/raw/cole-question-framework-buy-now.md`.

### Permission–Reason Why–Question (for personal/emotional reasons)

Don't ask "what would that do for you personally?" — it reads salesy. Instead:

> "Now, can I ask you a personal question? ... The reason I'm asking is, what's really
> important to me is not just helping you build a business that's building you wealth, but
> also one that's empowering you to live whatever lifestyle you want to live. So when you
> think about that — what is that for you? What comes up? What are the non-monetary goals,
> the personal goals, you want your business to allow you to achieve?"

- **Source:** [Cole-QFramework] / [Cole-7Step]
- **Why it works (4 compliance triggers):** "Now" = present-moment direct command;
  permission increases compliance; "personal question" presupposes a personal answer;
  reason-why framed in *their* benefit puts you on the same side of the table.

### Permission–Context–Question (for "why now" / moment of decision)

Paint their behavior as a mild abnormality, then have them justify it to you (leverages
the consistency bias):

> "Can I ask you a personal question? ... I mean, 10 years — after all that time, it's been
> a real career for you. I guess what I'm curious about is, why all of a sudden now though?
> What shifted for you — what happened?"

- **Source:** [Cole-QFramework]
- **Note:** Always tack on **"what happened"** — that's what surfaces the moment of decision.

### The "any other reason" question

Everyone has two reasons — the one that sounds good and the real one:

> "Hmm, I guess that makes sense... is there any other reason this is important to you now
> though?"

- **Source:** [Cole-QFramework]

### ESP — Exactly, Specifically, Precisely

A directing device (learned from Kevin Nations). "What questions do you have about the
process **specifically**?" / "Where do you feel you fall out **exactly**?" — directs their
attention where you want it. — [Cole-Webinar]

### "What are your thoughts on that?" vs "any thoughts?"

> "If you ask 'any thoughts on that' they say nope. If you ask 'what are your thoughts on
> that' they answer. There's an implied direct command in every question." — [Cole-Pt3]
