📖 Quick glossary, the words this doc uses
A bunch of sales words show up throughout. Here's plain English for each one so you don't have to guess. Skim it once now, come back if you forget.
  • Objection — anything the prospect says that means "I'm not buying right now." Examples: "let me think", "money's tight", "I need to talk to my partner", "I'm not sure this is for me." Pushback, basically.
  • Discovery — the part of the call where you ASK questions to find out what's really going on. The opposite of pitching. Crucially: you don't take the first answer. You go 3 to 6 layers deep on each question, because the first answer is the polite/safe one. The real answer (the one that closes the deal later) lives 3-6 follow-ups underneath.
  • Pillar — one of the 3 main points you make when you pitch your offer. Think of pillars as your 3 reasons "why my way works." Each pillar uses an analogy. Same as a "step" but with more punch.
  • Analogy — comparing something the prospect doesn't fully understand (your method) to something they already know (a thermostat, compound interest, a Michelin restaurant). Analogies make new ideas instantly clickable, because the prospect's brain doesn't have to build a new concept from scratch. It just attaches your idea to a concept they already trust. This is why every pillar in a great pitch is an analogy, not a feature list.
  • Close (verb) — to get them to actually buy. NOT "end the call." When we say "the close" we mean the moment they say yes and you take payment. With some added nuance: a clean close is unambiguous, the prospect either says yes or no, no "let me think about it" middle ground. Your job in the script is to drive toward a yes-or-no decision, not toward a vague "I'll get back to you."
  • Intent statement — a short line you say BEFORE asking a sensitive question that explains WHY you're asking. Without it, hard questions feel like an attack. With it, they feel like care. Example: "So that I can understand you better, what's going on in your head when you cancel each time?" The "so that I can understand you better" is the intent statement. It earns you permission to ask the harder thing right after. Trigger rule: use one before EVERY Cost question, every relationship/partner question, every "why" that touches identity. If you're about to ask something that'd feel rude at a dinner party, you need an intent statement.
  • Pre-frame — the 2 to 3 sentences you say BEFORE you pitch, to clear out the wrong mental picture the prospect is carrying in. "This is NOT a course where you watch videos and figure it out alone, it IS..."
  • Paradigm shift — fancy phrase for "the new way of thinking that replaces their old wrong belief." When you say "most diets fail because people treat their body like an engine, it's actually a thermostat", that's a paradigm shift.
  • Tie-down — a short check-in question that locks a small "yes" before you move on. The doc uses this word in TWO places: (1) the Pitch tie-down after each pillar — always TWO questions back-to-back: "Are you following me so far?" / "Make sense?" / "Following?" then immediately "What questions do you have about that part specifically?" The second question surfaces real friction; "Following?" alone gets a polite "yeah" and the doubt comes out later as "let me think." (2) The commitment tie-down in the objection handle's Step 5 — "If I solve [their concern], is there ANYTHING else that would stop you moving forward today? ... Okay. So if I solve that, you're 100% in tonight?" Same name, different jobs — both lock a small yes before any concession lands.
  • Tonality — your tone of voice. How you SAY something, not the words. Same sentence said cold vs warm lands completely differently.
  • Concerned-operator tone — sounding like a worried friend or a calm doctor, not a salesperson. Lower volume, slower pace, you genuinely care whether they solve their problem.
  • Downward inflection — your voice goes DOWN at the end of the sentence (like a period, not a question mark). Saying the price with a downward inflection = "It's $2,400." (voice drops ↓). Upward inflection = "It's $2,400?" (voice rises ↑) which signals uncertainty.
  • Diffuse → Isolate → Temp Check → Scale → Double tie-down — the 5-step recipe for handling any objection. You'll see this come up later. Diffuse = drop the pressure ("no problem"). Isolate = find out if there's anything else bothering them. Temp check = take their temperature on everything else. Scale = "1 to 10, where are you?" Double tie-down = "so if I solved this, you'd be in?"
  • Loop-back, as many layers as it takes — keep asking "why" as many times as it takes to hit the identity layer (usually 5+). "I want more clients." "Why?" "We need pipeline." "Why does that matter?" "I'm not hitting target." "And what happens if you keep missing?" "I'll have to lay off Sarah." "And what would that mean about you?" "I'd feel like a fraud." THAT last line is the real one. Notice the shift from BUSINESS consequence ("lay off Sarah") to IDENTITY consequence ("I'd feel like a fraud"). The real answer is always identity-level. That's how you know you've hit bottom — not a number of layers, an answer about who they are.
  • Thesis — the main idea behind your offer. The "reason your way works." If a prospect doesn't believe the thesis, no payment plan will close them.
  • Lay-down close — a sale where the prospect basically sells themselves. Zero objections, just yes. Rare, but they happen when the rest of the call goes really well.
  • LTV (lifetime value) — the total $ a typical client pays you across all the time they stay with you. Used in upside-math for B2B/revenue offers.
  • ICP (ideal customer profile) — the kind of person you want to sell to. Their industry, size, situation. The opposite of "anyone with a pulse."
  • DWY / DFY — DWY = Done With You (you do it together). DFY = Done For You (you do it for them). DIY = Do It Yourself (they do it alone, you just teach).

1. The 7 stages of a sales call, start to finish

Every high-ticket sales call moves through the same 7 stages. They go in order. Each one has a single job. Skip a stage or rush it, and the deal usually dies later in the call without you knowing why.

#StageWhat you're trying to do
1IntroductionThe first 60 seconds. Set the tone, set the rules, get them to agree to how the call will go. This is where most sales are actually won or lost, way before the price.
2DiscoveryAsk 9 specific kinds of questions (the DISCOVERY framework — D, I, S, C, O, V, E, R, Y) to find out what they actually want, what's actually broken, what staying stuck is costing them, and who else is involved in the decision. Cover all 9, in any order, before you move on. Talk 20%, listen 80%.
3TransitionThe bridge from "asking questions" to "telling them how you'd solve it." Summarise what you heard, then ask if they want to hear your plan. Don't pitch until they say yes.
4PitchExplain your method in 3 simple steps (we call them pillars). Each pillar is an analogy that shows WHY the way they've tried before didn't work, and WHY your way is different. After each one, ask "are you following me so far?" Don't list what they get. Sell the reason your way works.
5CommittingTake their temperature before money is on the table. Ask "does this feel like what you've been looking for?" Then "1 to 10, where are you?" Anything under 9, find out why. Then explain what onboarding looks like, then drop the price with your voice going DOWN at the end, then shut up and let them talk first.

Handle real doubt BEFORE you handle money.

2. What happens inside each stage

Each stage isn't one thing, it's a small sequence of moves. Here's what each stage actually contains:

  1. Introduction → small talk + your-day acknowledgement → pain acknowledgement → ideal-outcome question → permission to begin discovery.
  2. Discovery, the DISCOVERY framework (9 letters). Ask 9 specific kinds of questions (D, I, S, C, O, V, E, R, Y) to find out what they actually want, what's actually broken, what staying stuck is costing them, and who else is involved in the decision. Cover all 9, in any order, before you move on. Talk 20%, listen 80%. Real conversations jump around, follow where the prospect leads. The tool tracks which letters you've covered with a chip strip. Click each as you cover it. If you skip one, your review score takes the hit AND the missing letter usually shows up as the objection that kills the close.
    ↻ THE CORE DISCOVERY MOVE: Loop-back, as many layers as it takes (usually 5+)
    For EVERY one of the 9 letters, you don't take the first answer. You loop back by asking "why?" or "what do you mean by that?" or "and what's underneath that?" — as many times as it takes to hit the identity layer. The first answer is the polite/safe one. The real answer (the one that closes the deal later) usually lives 5+ follow-ups underneath. Keep going until you hit something IDENTITY-level (who they are, who they're not, who they're afraid of becoming) — not just another business/practical consequence.
    "I want more clients." → "Why?" → "We need pipeline." → "Why does that matter?" → "I'm not hitting target." → "And what happens if you keep missing?" → "I'll have to lay off Sarah." → "And what would that mean for you?" → "I'd feel like a fraud."

    THAT last line is the real reason. Now you have a North Star for the rest of the call.
    The 9 letters, in plain English, with the exact question to ask
    • D, Desire. What does this person actually want? Not the answer they give everyone ("more clients", "to lose weight"). The real one underneath. The reason they actually picked up the phone today. Keep asking "why does that matter to you?" until they tell you something personal. That's your North Star for the whole call.
      [CURIOUS TONE] "Just curious... if we DID get you to [their stated goal] in the next 6 months... what actually changes for you, day to day? ... (pause) ...and why does THAT matter to you?"
    • I, Issue. What's specifically broken right now? Not "marketing sucks", but "we book 6 calls a month and need 20." Real numbers. Real specifics. If they're being vague, keep asking "okay but what does that actually look like day to day?" until you get something concrete.
      [CURIOUS TONE] "Walk me through last week, specifically... what's actually NOT working right now? Like, give me a real example — not the version you'd give a friend who asked, the version that's actually true."
    • S, Sum. The numbers. If they're trying to make money, you need their average client value, how often they close, and how many calls they get a month. If they're trying to change their body, you need their starting point, their goal, and when. Write these down word-for-word, you'll need them later when you tell them how much you cost.
      [CURIOUS, MATTER-OF-FACT — three SEPARATE breaths, three pauses, three answers]
      Q1: "Just so I've got the picture... where are you right now on [the metric]?" (pause, let them fully answer)
      Q2: "Got it. And where do you want to be?" (pause, let them fully answer)
      Q3: "And by when?" (this is the one they'll skip if you stack it — let it land alone)
      Three forced commitments, not one rushed bundle. Stack them and the prospect answers the first and skips the timeline — you'll discover that gap at the price drop when "by when" becomes "oh I wasn't planning on this year." Three SEPARATE 2-second pauses between questions (the thinking-pause length), not one rushed bundle.
    • C, Cost. What does staying stuck actually cost them? Money. Time. Their relationship. Stuff they're missing. Don't tell them the cost. Get them to tell YOU the cost in their own words. The bigger the number they say out loud, the easier it'll be for them to pay you later, because you'll quote their own words back to them.
      [CONCERNED-CONFUSED TONE — half your normal volume, slower pace, downward inflection on "costing"]
      "Sarah, mind if I ask something a bit more direct... what's it actually costing you that this is still going on? ... (LONG pause — at least 4 seconds. Do NOT fill it. They were about to say the real thing.) ...and beyond that — what's it costing you on the personal side?"
      "Mind if I ask something a bit more direct" IS the intent statement, integrated. Cost has to land concerned — not curious. If you ask Cost in a curious voice it sounds clinical and they give you a clinical answer (money). Concerned tone + 4-second pause unlocks the personal-cost answer that closes the deal later. Stacking "financially, personally, all of it" lets them pick the easy lane (money) and skip the personal cost — which is the one you need.
    • O, Own. Why hasn't doing it on their OWN worked? They've tried things before. You need them to OWN that what they're doing isn't going to get them where they want to go by themselves. If they don't admit this out loud, they'll say "let me try one more thing on my own first" right at the price drop.
      [CURIOUS TONE] "Out of curiosity... what've you tried on your own up to this point? ... (pause, let them list) ...and like — what do you think it is about doing this on your OWN that hasn't quite gotten you there?"
      The second question has to put the agency back on THEM. "What stopped IT" gets program-blame answers ("Noom didn't work, the trainer was wrong fit"). "What is it about doing this on your own that hasn't gotten you there" pulls them into self-ownership — which is the whole point of O. Without that admission you'll get "let me try one more thing on my own" at price drop.
    • V, Verify. Do they actually agree with you, or are they just being polite? Test it. If they say "yeah I guess" in a flat voice, they don't agree. Find out what they actually think before you move on, because polite-nodding is the #1 reason people say "I need to think about it" at the end.
      [CONCERNED-CONFUSED TONE — like you genuinely might be wrong] "I want to make sure I've actually heard you and not just made up a story in my head... when you said [their EXACT words, not paraphrased], what I'm hearing underneath is [your diagnosis]. ... (4-second pause — this IS a mirror-back, give her the invitation length) ...where am I wrong on that?"
      "...correct?" invites the polite nod V is designed to expose. "Am I close?" still biases toward "yeah you got it." "Where am I wrong on that?" PRESUPPOSES you're partly wrong — now they have to either correct you (real engagement with the diagnosis) or affirm with their own conviction. Use their EXACT words when you restate, not your paraphrase — paraphrasing tells them you've already moved on.
    • E, Everyone. Who else has to say yes? Husband, wife, business partner, boss, parents. If anyone else has to approve this, you have to handle it NOW, not at the price drop. Otherwise you'll waste the whole call.
      [INTENT] "Quick one before we go further... when it comes to a decision like this, who else weighs in with you?"
      "Is there anyone else?" is a yes/no — they'll reflexively say "no." "Who else weighs in?" PRESUPPOSES someone does and forces a name. Once you have the name, THEN run the pre-close: "If they thought this was a great idea too, would you both be in?"
    • R, Resources. Can they actually afford this? Don't ask "can you afford it?", that's a yes/no question they'll answer without thinking. Ask "if we needed to start this week, what would your money situation look like?" Find out what they can actually pay so the price isn't a shock later.
      [CURIOUS TONE] "If we DID figure out this was the right fit and you wanted to get started this week... what'd that look like financially on your end? Like — walk me through what you'd be working with."
      "Would that be doable?" collapses into yes/no and you'll get a reflexive "yeah" with no actual numbers. "Walk me through what you'd be working with" forces them to describe their real cashflow situation — that's the data you need.
    • Y, Why now. What changed recently? People don't randomly book sales calls. Something happened. A bad month at work. A scary doctor's appointment. Their partner said something. Find out what that was. That thing IS the urgency, you don't have to make one up.
      [CURIOUS TONE] "Out of curiosity... what changed recently that made you decide to look at this NOW rather than 6 months ago? What was the moment?"
  3. Pitch — 3 pillars + double tie-down per pillar. Each pillar: a paradigm shift (why their past attempts failed) + proof (case study / data / story) + analogy + double tie-down ("Are you following me so far? ... What questions do you have about that part specifically?"). Two questions, not one — the first gets a polite yes, the second surfaces actual doubt before it kills the close. Pillar 1 reframes the MECHANISM (the wrong mental model they're using); Pillar 2 reframes the METHOD with a case study attached (proof); Pillar 3 is IDENTITY + future-pace tied to a real Discovery scene (who they become).
  4. Committing — 6 sub-stage steps: upside-math line → small temp-check (soft pre-money pulse: "does this feel like what you've been looking for?") → hard temp-check (1-10 scale: "where do you fall, and what's keeping you off a 9 or 10?") → smooth transition → onboarding line BEFORE price → price drop on a downward inflection → SILENCE.
  5. Objections — universal 5-step handle: diffuse ("no problem") → isolate ("aside from that, anything else?") → temp-check (is the rest 100% there?) → scale (1-10, why so low?) → double tie-down (commit them to the decision IF you solve it). Plus 34 objection-specific scripts the keyword engine pulls when a trigger phrase fires.
  6. Close confirmation → restate the next step → pre-frame buyer's remorse → start onboarding while still on the call.

3. The plays you use when the prospect says certain things

These aren't tied to a stage. They fire when the prospect says something specific that calls for them. The boxes below are the ones worth memorising, the rest are listed underneath.

Loop-back, 5 to 7 layers deep
The first answer to a discovery question is almost never the real one. Loop back and keep asking why until you hit what's underneath.
Why it matters
Surface answers protect the mask. The actual reason they took your call lives 3 to 5 layers down. Close on the surface story and you'll have no real lever for the price drop later. The deeper you go, the more they sell themselves.
How to run it
Prospect: "I just want more leads." You: "What about more leads though, why?" → "We're missing pipeline." → "Why's that pulling on you specifically?" → "I'm not hitting target." → "And why does that matter, beyond the number?" → "If I miss again my partner's going to ask if this business even works."
Drop tone at the end of each "why." Slow your pace. Make it feel like curiosity, not interrogation.
Trap to avoid: moving on after the first answer because it sounds reasonable. The reasonable answer is the mask. Push past it.
Concerned-operator tonality
Run the call FOR them, not TO them. Same words in a different tone land completely differently.
Why it matters
Tone is what gates whether they open up or armour up. Concerned-operator tone signals "I'm in this with you," not "I'm trying to close you." It's how you stay welcome in the awkward parts of the conversation, when you're asking the hardest questions.
How to run it
Lower your volume at the end of questions. Slow down. Let pauses sit. Imagine you're a friend who genuinely cares whether they solve this. That's the energy.
Trap to avoid: rep-energy. Fast pace, upward inflection, "great question!", excitement. That tells them you're selling. Pull all of that out.
Negative-identity flip
When the prospect uses minimising language ("happy with," "at least," "I'd settle for"), name it back and flip it.
Why it matters
Minimising language is how prospects pre-emptively lower the stakes of changing. If you accept it, you lose access to the real upside. Flip the identity and they re-engage with what they actually want.
How to run it
"You said you'd be happy with $20k more a month. What happens if we go for that and stop there, leave the other $50k on the table? ... You don't strike me as the type of person who wants the least out of this. Would I be right?"
Trap to avoid: jumping to the identity line without re-meaning what they said first. That comes off as glib flattery. Reframe their language, THEN lock the new meaning.
⚠️ DO NOT USE on: sensitive niches (health, grief, mental health, recently-burned buyers) — reads as coercive; first calls with strangers (no trust yet, lands as cheap manipulation); B2B professional services partners (sounds like pop-psychology, partner clocks the manoeuvre); non-native delivery (the "you don't strike me as" phrasing is brittle in a second language). This is an advanced move for buyers you have rapport with. Skip it on calls 1-5.
Catalyst event
Find out what shifted recently that made NOW the time to take this call. Then measure the gap between their current way and where they want to be.
Why it matters
Without a catalyst, there's no urgency. With a catalyst named out loud, "I'll think about it" becomes much harder for them to say. They sold themselves on the urgency.
How to run it
"People don't book a call for no reason. What shifted recently that made NOW the time to look at this? ... And with your current way of doing it, how close does that realistically get you to [their goal]?"
The gap they describe IS the urgency. You don't have to manufacture it.
Trade every concession for a decision
If you give ground, payment plan, scope tweak, free add-on, you ALWAYS trade it for a yes.
Why it matters
Concessions without a trade train the prospect that more concessions are available. Concessions with a trade close the deal. The math is simple: "I'll do X if you do Y."
How to run it
"If I'm willing to split this into 3 payments for you, will you move forward with me right now?"

Sensitive / consumer / B2B-partner variants: drop "yes or no, that's all I need" — it reads as debt-collector when delivered by a beginner or to anyone who isn't an already-warm buyer. The conditional ("if I'm willing to... will you...") IS the forced choice; you don't need to underline it twice.
Trap to avoid: dropping the concession with no commitment ask, hoping it builds goodwill. It doesn't. It tells them you'll keep negotiating.

Other tactics in the bag (less heavily coached but worth knowing the move name when the copilot fires them):

  • Re-meaning before identity-lock. Don't jump to "you're the type of person who…" yet. First reframe what they said, THEN lock the new meaning. Identity without re-meaning sounds like glib flattery.
  • Past-pain probe. "Take me back to the LAST time you tried to fix this, what specifically happened? What did it cost you?" Past pain makes them feel the future fear. You don't have to spell it out.
  • Handle uncertainty before logistics. If they're not 100% sold on the THESIS, no amount of payment-plan negotiating closes them. The tool sequences this automatically, uncertainty first, money/support/timing second.

3b. How to stop objections before they happen

Most people focus on handling objections at the end of the call. Top sellers do something smarter. They stop objections from forming in the first place. Half your "I need to think about it" moments can disappear if you spot the signals earlier and ask one good follow-up.

The simple rule. Objections happen when the prospect isn't sure. Not when they don't have enough info. People in research mode have lots of info and still don't buy. People who feel sure rarely object. Being sure isn't built by giving them a brochure, it's built by HOW you ask questions, when you pause, and the tone you use.

The hesitation probe
When you see a tone shift, a body-language close-off, or a "yeah sure" that lacks conviction, name it back in a soft concern tone and ask what's behind it.
Why it matters
Concerns that don't get aired in real time turn into objections at close, by which point they've hardened into a no. Probing the hesitation early lets you handle it while it's still a question, not a deal-breaker.
How to run it
"Hey [Name]... just gonna pause here for a sec. When I mentioned [X], it almost felt like something shifted. Am I reading that right, or am I off?"
Soft, slow, empathy tone. "Almost felt like something shifted" is less clinical than "you seemed a bit hesitant" — sounds less rehearsed. "Am I off?" gives them an easy out, which paradoxically makes them MORE honest. Avoid "what's behind that" — prospects can smell salesperson phrases.
Trap to avoid: firing this in the first 30 seconds, there's no trust yet, it lands as pressure. Use it from Discovery onward, once they're talking to you, not at you.
"Off the record" softener
Permission line that gives them cover to drop the mask and tell you the real thing.
Why it matters
Prospects perform on sales calls. They tell you what they think a buyer should say. The off-the-record line lowers that performance instinct, you're inviting them to be honest, and honesty is what you need to actually help them.
How to run it
"You can tell me what's actually going on here, don't worry, this is off the record."
Pair this with the hesitation probe for max effect. The combination, soft probe + off-the-record permission, gets you to the real concern fastest.
Listen to what they MEAN, not what they SAY
The tone IS the signal. When the words and the tone don't match, trust the tone.
Why it matters
"Yeah, sure, that sounds good" with a flat tone is not a yes. "For sure, this is exactly what we need" with full energy is a yes. Mid-call yes-es that don't match tone are how reps end up on follow-up calls that go nowhere.
How to run it
"Just gonna check in... you said yes, but the tone felt a bit different from where you were 2 minutes ago. What's actually going on for you right now?"
When the tone is weaker than the words, probe it before moving on. The contrast IS the probe — anchor on the baseline ("2 minutes ago") so they hear it themselves. Don't paper over a soft yes.

The 6 tonality categories (each one has a real-time trigger so you can spot WHEN to use it mid-call):

  • Curious. "Tell me more about that..." USE IT WHEN: you want them to elaborate without feeling pushed. Trigger: any time they give you a 1-sentence answer to a question you wanted more on.
  • Concern (empathy). Lower volume, slower pace, soft. USE IT WHEN: asking a sensitive question (Cost, Own, anything touching relationship or money) — or right after they share something hard.
  • Confused. "Wait, when you said X, what do you mean exactly?" USE IT WHEN: re-meaning or loop-back. Trigger: any word they used that you don't fully understand, or a phrase that sounds rehearsed/scripted (force them to define it in their own words).
  • Challenging. "You don't strike me as the type of person who wants the least." USE IT WHEN: doing a negative-identity flip. Trigger: minimising language ("happy with", "at least", "I'd settle for").
  • Playful. Light, smile-in-voice delivery. USE IT WHEN: deploying the off-the-record softener, the 1-10 scale humor ("I hate this guy, get me off the phone"), or any moment you need to break tension after a heavy beat.
  • Concerned-confused. A blend — sounds like "I genuinely don't understand what you mean, and I'm a little worried about you." Quieter, slower, slight uplift on the question. USE IT WHEN: any loop-back layer 3+, the Verify question ("where am I wrong on that?"), the Cost identity-layer question, the definitive-100% question, the partner double tie-down, the burned-before isolate, any moment you want a hard answer without the prospect feeling interrogated. This is the most-used elite tonality in the whole call. If you only learn one new tone, learn this one.
    Where NOT to use it: the negative-identity flip (needs CHALLENGING, not soft), the price drop (needs flat/downward, not confused), the tie-down "following me?" (needs warm-curious), Step 1 "no problem" diffuse (needs flat-low, not worried). Concerned-confused is for diagnostic questions — not assertions, not transitions, not commitments.
Pause length is a deliberate lever — 2 / 4 / 7 second rule
Pauses are named throughout this doc ("3-second pause", "long pause"). Don't guess the length. Each duration does a different job.
2 seconds = thinking pause. Signals you're processing what they said. Use after any answer that needs a follow-up question.
4 seconds = invitation pause. They will fill it with more truth. Use after any heavy beat (Cost identity-layer answer, mirror-back of their exact words, husband/partner reveals). 4 seconds feels uncomfortably long. That's the point.
7+ seconds = pressure pause. Only after the price drop or a definitive question. Whoever talks first owns the next move. Count to 10 in your head if you have to.
Trap to avoid: filling a 4-second invitation pause at 1.5 seconds because the silence feels rude. It isn't rude. They were about to say the real thing. Beginners cut all their pauses in half under pressure. Pros set a mental stopwatch.
The ledge — unpolished delivery before a hard question
Before any high-stakes question (Cost identity-layer, the definitive 100% question, the partner double tie-down, the burned-before isolate), the elite move is a verbal stumble that signals you're about to ask something you're a little uncomfortable asking. This is on purpose. Polished asks sound scripted. Unpolished asks sound real — and real asks earn real answers.
Ledge phrases (pick one, vary across the call)
"This might be a weird question, but..."
"I don't know if this is fair to ask, but..."
"Hmm. ...can I just check something?"
"Mind if I ask something a bit more direct?"
"Sorry — one more thing, just so I understand..."
The slight stumble signals real-time thinking, which signals you're not running a script, which paradoxically gives you permission to ask the hardest question on the call. Intent statements are the polished version of a ledge. The ledge is the unpolished version. Both work — vary them so you don't sound formulaic.
Trap to avoid: using the same ledge three times in one call. "This might be a weird question, but..." x3 = scripted. Pick a different ledge each time. Or alternate with a plain intent statement.
Myth-buster: more objections does NOT mean more interest. It's the opposite. Fewer objections = more certainty triggered. Lay-down closes have zero objections, not five. If you're getting a lot of objections at the end, you missed something in Discovery, almost always a DISCOVERY letter you didn't cover properly. The chip strip is your prevention system.
Your first 5 calls — the 80/20 starter pack (read this if the doc is overwhelming you)
This whole doc is 2,000+ lines because it's the operator's manual. On call #1, you don't need 9 letters AND 6 tones AND 7 beliefs AND 3 pause lengths AND 5-step handle all at once. You need 4 things, perfectly. Master these on calls 1-5. Then come back and layer in the rest.
The 4 must-haves
1) The opener. "Hey [Name], tell me about your day." Then: "Before we dive in, by the end of this call, what's the one thing that would make it a great use of your time?" That's it. Two lines. Memorise them.

2) The 4 critical Discovery letters: D, I, C, V. Forget the other 5 for your first calls. Just cover Desire (what do they actually want?), Issue (what's specifically broken?), Cost (what's it costing them — ask ONCE, normal voice), Verify ("where am I wrong on that?"). Skip Sum, Own, Everyone, Resources, Why-now until call 6+.

3) Two micro-tie-downs after each pillar. "Following me?" → wait → "What questions do you have about that part specifically?" Two questions back-to-back. That's the whole tonality lesson for now.

4) The price drop + silence. "And [offer] is $[X], or [Y] payments of $[Z]. Which works better for you?" — then SHUT UP for 10 seconds. Count to 10 in your head. Whoever talks first owns the next move.
If you do ONLY these 4 things on call #1, you'll close more than 80% of what a fully-trained rep would close. The other moves in this doc compound from here.
What to SKIP entirely on calls 1-5
✗ The 6 tonality categories (just be calm + curious, that's enough)
✗ The 7 Beliefs map (advanced diagnostic, not a script)
✗ Open Wallet, the Tomorrow seed, the negative-identity flip (high-risk moves — earn them later)
✗ The "yes or no, that's all I need" hard close (sounds like a debt collector when you're a beginner)
✗ Sum / Resources / Everyone DISCOVERY letters (cover Desire/Issue/Cost/Verify first; layer the rest on call 6+)
✗ The 2/4/7 pause lever (just leave longer silences than feels comfortable — that's enough)
Trap to avoid: trying to run all 9 DISCOVERY letters + 6 tones + 5-step handle on your first call. You'll be in your head the entire time and the prospect will feel it. Master the 4 must-haves on 5 calls. THEN read this whole doc again and layer in the rest. The doc is a buffet, not a checklist.

3c. The money moments, where most calls are won or lost

These are the parts of the call that decide whether you make money or not. Each box explains the move in plain English, why it matters, the exact words to use, and the trap to avoid.

Sell the method, NOT the deliverables
In Pitch, sell the WHY your way works. The deliverables (calls, modules, support, sessions) describe shape. The method earns the price.
Why it matters
Reps who list deliverables get commoditised, the prospect compares your weekly calls to someone else's daily calls and picks on price. Reps who sell the method get bought, because no one else has YOUR method. The method is unique to you. The deliverables are not.

🧠 Remember it this way: like a chef selling the recipe vs the ingredients. Anyone can buy flour. Only YOU have the recipe.
How to run it
Each pillar = a paradigm shift (why their past attempts failed) + the better way (your method) + proof (case study, your own story, or the reason-why). Don't say "you get 12 weekly calls." Say "we run a weekly diagnostic until X, because the way most coaches teach this is the reason none of it sticks."
Trap to avoid: the deliverables list. The moment you start describing WHAT they get, stop and pivot back to WHY it works.
How to actually WRITE your 3 pillars (the architecture)
Pillars aren't random steps. There's a structure. Get this right once when you write My Offer and every Pitch you ever run lands harder.
The "us vs them" frame, the core of every pillar
Each pillar simultaneously explains (a) what most people do wrong, (b) the consequence of doing it that way, (c) what YOU do instead, (d) the result they get. That's "us vs them." It positions YOU as different from the market, and it positions THEM as needing you (because they've been doing it the wrong way).
"You know how most [niche] people [common behaviour]? The problem with that is [reason it fails], which is why you ended up [consequence they actually told you in Discovery]. So the first thing we do instead is [your better approach], so that [benefit], which means [identity-level benefit-of-the-benefit]. Are you following me so far? ... What questions do you have about that part specifically?"
Why this template: the bracket "[consequence they actually told you in Discovery]" forces you to plug in HER OWN words. That's what stops your pillar sounding generic. The double tie-down "Are you following me so far? ... What questions do you have about that part specifically?" is non-negotiable — the first question gets a polite yes, the second surfaces actual friction. Drop the second question and you'll find the doubt at "let me think about it" instead of mid-pillar where you can still handle it.
Trap to avoid: naming competitors. "Us vs them" attacks the CATEGORY's failed mental model ("most coaches treat the body like an engine"), not a named competitor ("unlike Noom..."). The moment you name a competitor you sound petty. Stay categorical.
Feature → Benefit → Benefit-of-the-benefit (always go to layer 3)
Feature alone = boring. Feature + benefit = OK. Feature + benefit + benefit-of-the-benefit = persuasive. Stop at layer 2 and your pillars feel flat.
Feature: "We do a weekly diagnostic call" → Benefit: "so your plan stays calibrated to what's actually happening in your body" → Benefit-of-the-benefit: "which means you never hit the wall most people hit at week 6 where everything stops working and they quit."
Pillar 1 = MECHANISM. Pillar 2 = METHOD + proof. Pillar 3 = IDENTITY + future-pace.
The split is sharper than it looks. Pillar 1 reframes the MECHANISM — the wrong mental model they're operating on (body-as-engine, marketing-as-spray-and-pray, symptoms-as-enemy). Their old method couldn't work because their model was wrong. Pillar 2 reframes the METHOD + drops the case study — your specific way of doing it that produces a different result, with a real client name + before/after attached as proof. Pillar 3 = identity + future-pace tied to a real scene from their Discovery (the beach birthday she cancelled, the meeting she dreaded, the family event she missed). If Pillar 1 and Pillar 2 feel interchangeable, you've written two of the same pillar — go back and make P1 about the mental model, P2 about the method-with-proof. Watch out for collapsing Pillar 3 into another benefit — Pillar 3 isn't "you'll get X result", it's "you become someone who…" tied to a real moment they told you about.
How to NAME your pillars (this matters more than you think)
Don't invent jargon ("the Black Box System", "the Velocity Method"). Anyone with half a brain hears that and tunes out. Instead, use a common term that sounds slightly new in your context. "Maintenance number" beats "Metabolic Optimisation Protocol." "Outbound system" beats "Pipeline Catalyst Framework." Familiar word + your context = new without being cringe.
Pitch is a DIALOGUE, not an anvil drop
Bad pitch: "save your questions till the end" then 8 minutes of monologue. Good pitch: after every pillar, "does that make sense? What questions do you have about that specifically?" Get them to verbally agree with each pillar before moving on. Each "yeah that makes sense" is a micro-commitment that makes the eventual yes inevitable. Aim for a back-and-forth, not a presentation.
Trap to avoid: writing pillars in a vacuum. After you fill in My Offer once, run a fake call against it. If your own pillar reads boring back to you, it'll read boring to the prospect. Rewrite until you'd buy it.
Every pillar carries 3 ingredients — Method + Mechanism + Why-different
Humans understand things through contrast. Your pillar must simultaneously explain why everything they've tried before failed (mechanism) AND why your way is different (method + result). They get an "aha" moment as you pitch — you're teaching while you sell.
The 3 ingredients of every pillar
Problem-based mechanism: WHY their past attempts failed — the underlying problem they didn't know they had.
Method: what YOU specifically do (your action).
Why this is different: the result your method produces that their old method couldn't.
Worked example — weight loss
"You haven't been able to lose weight because of insulin resistance. (mechanism) The way we fix that is by getting your body into ketosis, which is a fat-burning state. (method) Once you're in ketosis, your body actually burns fat instead of fighting to hold onto it — which is why every calorie-cut diet you've ever tried failed. (why this is different)"
Notice the prospect's "aha" — they finally understand why nothing else worked AND why this will be different, simultaneously. That's insight selling. Give them something they didn't already have on the call, and your offer becomes the obvious next step to act on the insight.
Worked example — agency growth
"You haven't been able to scale past $50k/mo because every new client adds workload back onto YOU specifically. That's the bottleneck. (mechanism) What we do is build a delivery team with SOPs and a hiring loop so every new client adds capacity, not workload. (method) Once you're not the bottleneck, growth compounds — every new hire pays for the next two. That's why hiring more contractors didn't fix it before; you needed a SYSTEM, not more bodies. (why this is different)"
Trap to avoid: teaching the mechanism but skipping the why-different. They'll nod along, agree the mechanism is real, then say "let me think about it" later. If you don't connect mechanism → method → result, the pillar is interesting but not buyable.
Reading the temp check, the tonality test before money
After your pitch + delivery summary, you'll do a temp check. The WORDS the prospect says matter less than HOW they say it. This is the moment to read the room.
What "certain" sounds like (drop the price next)
Fast cadence, full energy, no hesitation. "Dude, absolutely." "Yeah, this is exactly what I need." "100%, when do we start?" Tonality matches words. Go to onboarding + price drop.
What "uncertain" sounds like (do NOT drop the price)
Slow cadence, lower energy, hedge words. "I think so." "Yeah... I mean... probably." "It sounds good but..." Tonality says no even when words say yes. Probe before moving on.
"Cool, no problem. Just so I can stay organised on my end, when you say you think so, on a scale of 1 to 10, one being 'I hate this guy, get me off the phone' (deliver this with humour) and 10 being 'that's exactly what I need', where do you fall? ... What's keeping you from being a 9 or 10?"
The scale rules of thumb
9 or 10 = sale, go to price. 8 = close, ask "what gets you to a 9 or 10?" and handle the gap. 7 or under = something real is missing, isolate it BEFORE pricing. The first sale you have to make is them being certain this is the right thing AND now is the right time. If the thesis isn't sold, the price won't close.
Trap to avoid: dropping the price on a 7 or 8 hoping the number will pull them up. It won't. They'll politely object and you'll lose the deal. Never price a soft yes.
Tie-down after every pillar
After each of your 3 pillars, get an "are you following me so far?" Then listen for a 1-line answer in under 10 seconds.
Why it matters
Tie-downs catch confusion early so you don't end the pitch with a prospect who lost you at pillar 1. They also build small yes momentum, each tie-down is a micro-commitment that makes the eventual price-drop easier.
How to run it
"Are you following me so far? ... What questions do you have about that specifically?"
If the answer is longer than 10 seconds, they're avoiding or confused, loop until tight before moving on.
Trap to avoid: stacking all 3 pillars then asking for questions at the end. By then they've forgotten pillar 1.
After Pitch — handling questions ("great question. what else?")
Once you've stacked your 3 pillars, you don't go straight to "so the price is...". You stay in Committing and invite questions. How you handle each question decides whether the rest of the call slides into a yes or stalls. The goal is clear, honest communication — confused minds don't buy, clarity is king.
The opener after pillar 3
"So that's it — pillar 1, pillar 2, pillar 3. Ultimately that's set up so you can go from [where they are now] to [where they want to be]. So what questions do you have about those three things specifically?"
The 3-step framework for ANY question they ask
1) Clarify before you answer. Don't assume you know what they mean. "Is this group coaching?" might mean "I'm worried I won't get personal attention" OR "I had a bad group experience before." Clarify, or you'll answer the wrong question and they'll keep comparing your offer to whatever they had in mind.

2) Answer briefly — 2 sentences or less. The longer you talk, the less certain you sound. Short, definitive answer beats long-winded reassurance every time.

3) Then loop: "Great question. What else? What other questions do you have?" If you stop after one answer, the unspoken concerns turn into "let me think about it" at the price drop.
Example — "is this group coaching?"
"Gotcha — there are some group elements. Just so I can answer exactly what you mean: is there a specific reason you're asking? What are you looking for?"

(They say: "I just don't want to get lost in a big group.")

"Got it. Here's how it works: [your specific structure — X 1-on-1s, Y group sessions, Z direct access]. Most clients get more access than they expect."

"Great question. What else? What other questions do you have?"
Example — "how does the tech work?"
"Cool — so if I'm understanding you correctly, your question is (A) what software do you need, (B) who sets it up, and (C) [whatever's the third part]?"

(They confirm.)

"Great. (A) [answer]. (B) [answer]. (C) [answer]. ... Great question. What other questions do you have?"
Re-stating their question as A/B/C does two things: shows you actually heard them, and lets you answer it cleanly instead of rambling through three things at once.
When they say "no questions" — small temp check
"Gotcha. So — based on you telling me you want to get to [their outcome], do you feel like this is what you've been looking for?"
99% of the time they say yes. Full energy yes → run the hard temp check next. Hesitation → you missed something, go back to whichever DISCOVERY letter is weakest before you go near price.
Transition to price (the bridge)
"Great — so no questions, this is what you need. So where do you want to go from here?"

(They almost always say: "Yeah, let's do it / what's next?")

"Cool. [Offer name] is $[X] paid in full, or [Y] payments of $[Z]. Which works better for you?"
The bridge does 2 things: (1) confirms zero unanswered questions, (2) hands the steering wheel to them ("where do you want to go from here?"). Their "yeah, what's next?" IS the readiness check — you don't need a second "you ready?" gate, that just gives them a free exit ramp. Cut the word "just" before the price. "The investment is just $X" reads as YOU thinking the price needs minimising. Say the number flat, on a downward inflection, on a period — then shut up.
Trap to avoid: answering the FIRST question and immediately moving to price. They had one out-loud question — they probably have 2-3 unspoken ones. The "what else?" loop surfaces them before the price lands, not after.
The temp check, two moves: SMALL then HARD
Before money is on the table, take their temperature TWICE in a row. Different questions, different jobs. Don't skip from one to the other, run them as a sequence.
Why it matters
The small check is a soft "does this match?" pulse. The hard check forces them to actually commit a number out loud. Together they reveal whether you're at 9 (sale incoming) or 6 (a belief is missing and you need to go back, not drop price). One without the other misses information you'll need to close.
SMALL temp check, first move (soft pulse)
"Based on you telling me you want to get to [their outcome], does this feel like what you've been looking for?"
Listen to TONE more than words. "Dude, absolutely" with full energy = certainty, go to hard check. "Yeah I think so" with flat tone = uncertainty, you've missed something, go back to whichever DISCOVERY letter is weakest before you scale them.
HARD temp check, second move (force a number)
"On a scale of 1 to 10, where one is 'get me off the phone' and ten is 'that's exactly what I need,' where do you fall? ... What's keeping you from being a 9 or 10?"
9 or 10 = ready, run onboarding + price drop next. 8 = "what's keeping you from a 9?", handle it, re-ask. 7 or under = there's an unsolved belief or objection. Loop back to Discovery (whichever DISCOVERY letter is weakest) BEFORE you go near money. There are no 7s — a 7 is a polite no. Keep working until they say 9 or 10 out loud, or end the call honestly. Never price a 7.
Trap to avoid: skipping straight from pitch to price drop because "they seem keen." Without both temp checks you don't know if the keenness is real or polite, and you'll discover the gap right after the price lands. Run both, every time.
Price drop on a downward inflection, then silence
When you reveal the price, your tone goes DOWN at the end. Then say nothing. Let them respond first.
Why it matters
Downward inflection signals certainty. Upward inflection signals you're asking permission for the price, which gives them permission to say no. The silence after is non-negotiable, whoever talks first owns the next move. If you talk first, you'll discount. If they talk first, they'll buy or object honestly.
How to run it
"And [offer name] is $[X] paid in full, or [Y] payments of $[Z]. Which works better for you?" End on the period. Then stop. Don't fill the silence. If they say "let me think," go to the objection handle, don't repeat the price softer.
Trap to avoid: rushing to fill the pause. The four sentences that cost you the deal: "I know it's an investment but…" / "We can probably do something on the price…" / "A lot of my clients say it's worth it…" / "What are you thinking?" Each one signals YOU aren't sure of the price. Say the number. Stop. Count to 10 in your head if you have to.

3d. After the price drop — handling objections the right way

Goal: get to the truth. Every move below is built to surface what the prospect actually thinks, not what they're being polite about. Clear and honest communication. Confused minds don't buy. Clarity is king. Your job after the price drop is not to push, not to beg, not to sell a fantasy — just ask the question that flushes out the truth, sit with the answer, and respond from certainty. Are they in sync with you right now? Can they visualise this in their mind? If not, that's the work.

⚠️ Sensitive-niche red-list (READ FIRST): If you sell to chronically ill, grieving, mental-health, recently-burned, or otherwise emotionally vulnerable buyers, the following §3d moves are off-limits as written and must be replaced with their sensitive-niche variants in §3 → "Sensitive-niche swaps":
  • Cost identity-mining with 4-second weaponised pause — instead, ask once in normal voice, take whatever answer they give, move on.
  • O = Own self-blame extraction ("what is it about doing this on your OWN…") — replace with "what's already worked for you, even partially? What do you wish you'd had alongside it?"
  • Open Wallet credit / cash-on-hand sequence — instead, present payment plan + sliding scale, let them choose privately.
  • The definitive 100% question — replace with "how does this sit with you right now? Not yes-or-no — just honestly, how does it sit?"
  • Both Tomorrow seeds — replace with "take all the time you need. If it's right, you'll know. If you decide it's not, that's a real answer."
  • Negative-identity flip — do not run.
  • Mirror-loop past 2 layers on grief content — once is enough. Mining for more loss when someone is already in chronic pain is induced shame, not Discovery.

The principle: don't mine grief. Your job is to be honest with THEM, including when this isn't right for them. If you spot any of the below, end the call gently and offer a free resource instead of selling:

  • Active mental-health crisis or recent traumatic loss (within 4 weeks)
  • Recent diagnosis they're still processing
  • Mid-flare / acute-phase chronic illness
  • Financial distress where your offer would make things demonstrably worse
  • The prospect explicitly says "I can't make this decision right now, my brain isn't working" — believe them.
The 7 Beliefs — what must be true for them to buy
A prospect doesn't say yes to your offer until they've internally said yes to seven separate beliefs. Miss one in Discovery and that one shows up at the price drop as the objection that kills the deal.
The list
1) Pain or unfulfilled desire — "no pain or unmet desire, no gain, no more talking." If there's no real pain and no real outcome they want, the call ends here.
2) Doubt in themselves — they need to believe they can't solve this alone. If they think they can DIY it, they won't buy.
3) Cost of not solving it — staying stuck must cost more than your offer (financially, personally, identity-wise).
4) Belief in YOUR method — they need to believe YOUR way will get them from where they are to where they want to be.
5) Money — they have access to money AND are willing to spend it on this category.
6) Support — anyone who can veto (partner, board, CFO, family) is either on board, or it's truly the prospect's call alone.
7) Trust — they trust YOU specifically can deliver on the promise.
Map to DISCOVERY: the 9 DISCOVERY letters surface these 7 beliefs naturally. Issue + Desire = belief 1. Solutions (what have you tried + why didn't it work) = belief 2. Cost = belief 3. Verify + your Pitch = belief 4. Resources = belief 5 (money — access + willingness to spend). Everyone = belief 6. Why-this-call + Sum + your delivery in Pitch = belief 7 (trust + the math working). If an objection lands after the price drop, ask yourself which belief is missing? — that tells you what to actually handle.
Trap: handling the surface words of the objection without diagnosing the missing belief. "I need to think about it" is a symptom. The disease is one of the 7. Find which one and you handle the real thing.
Uncertainty vs Logistics — the first split after any objection
Every objection is one of two things. Either they're not 100% sold (uncertainty), OR they're 100% sold but something practical is in the way (logistics). You handle them differently — mix them up and you'll handle the money problem of someone who isn't even sold yet, and lose the deal twice.
Uncertainty (handle FIRST)
Anything that keeps them less than 100% certain that this is the right thing AND now is the right time. Examples: "I need to think," "I need to read more reviews," "let me talk to your clients," "send it to me in writing," "let's do this next quarter," "it's a little too much, I just don't know if I can make this work."
Logistics (handle SECOND, only after uncertainty is fully cleared)
They 100% want to do it — they just need to physically make it work. Two flavours: support-based ("I need to check with my partner / CEO / board, but I'm in") or financial ("I'm in, I just need to move money around / wait two weeks for X to clear / sort the cash flow").
The rule: ask the question that exposes a soft yes
[CONCERNED-CONFUSED TONE — slow, quiet, like you're trying to understand, not interrogate]
"Just so I can understand where you're really at... do you feel like this is 100% going to work for you, AND that this is 100% what you want to do? ... (4-second pause — don't fill it)"
Listen for the tonality of their answer more than the words. A definitive yes sounds like "oh no, I 100% want to do this" or "yes, this is exactly what I need" — full energy, no hedge. Anything wishy-washy ("yeah I think so," "probably," "it sounds great") is uncertainty wearing politeness. You're not commanding honesty. You're asking the question that makes their own hesitation audible — to them, not just to you.
Trap: delivering this hard, fast, or accusatory. "No bullshit on this call" is your INTERNAL posture, never your spoken energy. If your voice tightens, the prospect will match you and clamp shut. The whole move only works delivered concerned-curious — almost reluctant to ask. Before you ask it, exhale once silently. Your voice cannot stay concerned-operator if your chest is locked.
Trap: hearing "I need to talk to my partner" and immediately running the partner handle. 80% of the time partner is a smokescreen for uncertainty. Always run the uncertainty isolation FIRST.
The 5-step objection handle (your default for uncertainty)
Diffuse → Isolate → Temp check → Scale → Double tie-down. Same sequence every time. Use this whenever the objection looks like uncertainty (which it usually is, even when it sounds like money or partner).
Why it matters
Most reps go straight to "let me handle that for you" the moment an objection lands. That signals pressure and the prospect doubles down. The 5-step sequence diffuses pressure first, then isolates the real issue, then locks them back on certainty.
How to run it
1) DIFFUSE: "No problem." (drops the pressure, shows you're not pushing)
2) ISOLATE: (ledge) "Hmm. ...can I just check something? Before we get into that — you said you want to get to [their goal]. Do you feel like this is absolutely what you need to get there, AND that the approach itself is going to work for you?"
3) TEMP CHECK: "Aside from that, how do you feel about the rest of what we've discussed?"
4) SCALE: "1-10, where are you? ... What's keeping you from a 9 or 10?"
5) DOUBLE TIE-DOWN: "If I could solve [their concern] right now, is there ANYTHING else, even something small, that would stop you moving forward today? ... Okay. So if I solve that, you're 100% in tonight?"
Order matters in Step 5: the first question catches hidden objections. The second LOCKS the commitment BEFORE you offer any concession (payment plan, guarantee, scope tweak). Never trade a concession for "I think so" or "maybe" — only for a clear "yes if you solve that." 🧠 Remember it this way: a concession in exchange for a decision — never a concession in exchange for a maybe.
For sensitive niches (health, grief, mental health): the isolate line can sound clinical. Soften it: "Is it that you're not sure this approach is right for you, or is it more about figuring out the logistics (time, money, family)?" Same job, warmer tone.
Trap: handling the logistic before the uncertainty. If they say "it's too expensive but also I need to think," handle the THINK first. Money is a smokescreen while uncertainty is unresolved.
"I need to think about it" → the Tomorrow seed
When they say "send it to me, I'll think about it" and you've isolated that it IS uncertainty (not a logistic), you have one move: get them to commit to tomorrow being a real decision, not another stall.
The script (sequence)
"No problem. Just so I can be clear — based on you telling me you want to get to [their goal], do you feel like this is 100% what you need to be able to get there?"

(If wishy-washy:) "Got it. So what I'm really trying to understand is — do you not think this is 100% going to work, OR do you not 100% want to do this?"

(If they STILL want to sleep on it after that — drop the Tomorrow seed. Two versions, pick by risk-tolerance and offer terms:)

Binding version (assumptive close): "Here's what I'd suggest — let's lock the payment in tonight. You sleep on it. We don't need a second call to decide, we just start tomorrow. If nothing changes overnight, we're already moving. ...are you in?"

Softer version (use for sensitive niches or no-refund offers): "Cool — let's schedule a 5-minute payment call for tomorrow at [time], not another decision call. If we're having this exact same conversation tomorrow and you're feeling the exact same way you feel right now, we're 100% processing the payment and moving forward. Are we agreed on that?"
Key principle: the next call has to be administrative, not a re-decision. "I'll think about it and call you tomorrow to decide" is the stall pattern this doc trains you to crush — don't accidentally schedule one yourself. Either lock the commitment NOW with an honest exit window (binding version) OR frame tomorrow as the payment call (softer version). If they hesitate at either version → that's the real objection leaking out, isolate it now while they're still on the call.
Advanced — the line in the sand (only when they're right there)
"Look, you told me you want [outcome A] and you want to get away from [pain B]. Let's draw the line in the sand and make a commitment today. If you wake up tomorrow feeling like 'oh my god this was the worst thing in the world, the pit of my stomach knows I made a terrible decision' — we will refund you."
Only deploy when (a) you have a refund policy that backs it, AND (b) you can read they'll respond to certainty rather than feel cornered. If they're sensitive (health, grief, recently burned), skip this — schedule the follow-up and seed the homework instead.
Trap: deploying the line-in-the-sand on a sensitive buyer. Sounds like pressure. Use the gentle Tomorrow-seed path — schedule, give homework, trust the seed.
"I need to come up with the money" → Open Wallet
First confirm it's a logistic, not uncertainty wearing a money mask. Then have an honest financial conversation — not to pressure, but to figure out IF there's a way to make it work now, or build a real game plan for later.
Confirm it's actually the money
"Just so I'm clear — are you telling me that money aside, you're 100% in?"
Definitive yes → run Open Wallet. Anything else → back to uncertainty handle.
The frame (before you ask anything financial)
"Look — most of our clients do it upfront. For certain situations, we break it up. Are you open to having an honest conversation financially, getting everything on the table, so we can see IF there's a way we can make this work now — or at the very least, build a game plan together to make this work in the future?"
You're not selling here. You're diagnosing. They've already said yes to wanting it — this is just math, business-owner-to-business-owner.
The Open Wallet sequence (the 4 questions)
1) Incoming cash next 30 days: "Based on what you've got coming in minus expenses, what's left in 30 days?" (warm-up; the number itself doesn't matter much)

2) Cash on hand right now (be specific): "And what's actually in your checking account right now? Not what you can spend — I'm asking what you have access to."

3) If they offer a smaller "spendable" number ("I can only spend $500"): "I hear you — but I'm not asking what you can spend right now, and I don't even know if you can do this right now. I'm asking what's actually accessible. What's in checking?"

4) Credit: "And what's your available credit across cards, line of credit, or anywhere else you could access if you wanted to?"
The framing line that makes Open Wallet not feel weird: "You've told me three times you want to do this — it's only weird if you've changed your mind." Use that if they hesitate at question 2 or 3.
The deal-make (concession with terms)
"Look — I don't think the best thing for you right now is to do this all upfront if that'll zero you out or take half your net worth. I also don't think the best thing is to do nothing, based on what we've talked about. So what I'd be willing to do — if we split it into 2, half now and half in 30 days, business-owner to business-owner... if I'm willing to do that for you, will you move forward right now?"
Key phrase: "if I'm willing to do that for you, will you move forward right now?" — concession with terms in exchange for a decision. You're not offering a payment plan, you're trading one. Aim for 30% down minimum so they can actually start. If you have to drop to a 3-pay, get a video case-study commitment in exchange.
Trap: offering the payment plan BEFORE you've isolated uncertainty + done Open Wallet. You're giving away terms to someone who isn't sold, and you'll still lose the deal. Sequence: uncertainty → confirm money logistic → Open Wallet → deal-make with terms.
"I need to talk to my partner" → double tie-down + collaboration test
Partner is the most-faked objection on Earth. 80% of the time it's uncertainty hiding as politeness. Your job: isolate whether it's uncertainty or a real collaboration, AND if real, whether it's a "let them know" (decision made, just informing) vs a true "we decide together" (collaboration).
The double tie-down
[CONCERNED-CONFUSED TONE — soft, almost confused, not interrogating]
"No problem. ...this might be a weird question, but — based on your goal of [their outcome], for YOU personally, is this something you completely want to do?"

(If yes:) "Cool. So just to clarify — if this were solely your decision, we'd be processing the payment right now and you'd be 100% moving forward?"
Anything but "absolutely, this would be done" = uncertainty in disguise. Handle that first before you go anywhere near partner logistics.
Is it "let them know" or a real "we decide together"?
"Cool — so is this something where you've basically made the decision and you just need to let them know? Or is this something you guys really need to collaborate on?"
"Let them know" = sale is yours, just timing. "Collaborate" or anything ambiguous = back to Discovery for the partner.
Discovery on the partner (surface the landmines)
"What do you think they're going to think? ... What's their reaction going to be to the price specifically? ... If anything, what's the reason they wouldn't want you to do this?"
You're surfacing landmines BEFORE the partner conversation. From here you decide: do they just need to inform, do they need a pep-talk from you, OR does the partner actually need to be on a call? If "really on a call" — schedule a tight joint call within 48h, never an email recap.
Trap: letting them "go talk to their partner" without the double tie-down. Email recap = the deal dies on the kitchen table. If they're truly sold AND truly need partner, get the joint call on the calendar before you hang up.
"I've been burned before" → apples-to-oranges
Solution-based objection. Someone in your category overpromised and underdelivered, and now they're projecting that experience onto you. Your job: empathise, isolate that THIS is the only thing in the way, then run a side-by-side comparison that makes "the burned experience" and "you" look like different species.
Empathise + isolate
[CONCERNED-CONFUSED TONE]

Double tie-down (isolate the burn cleanly)

"So you're comparing someone who had [their numbers — usually small / new / unproven] to someone who's [your reasons-why — track record, client count, years in business, named results]. How does that comparison even make sense?"

"I'm sorry that happened. But the lesson there is don't work with the beginner — it's NOT 'don't ever do this kind of work again.' Don't learn the wrong lesson. Investing in the right help is part of the game. The person who hurt you proved you needed someone better, not no one at all."
Trap: shitting on the competitor by name. Stay categorical ("a coach with 5 clients vs someone who's done this for a decade"). The moment you name names you sound petty AND they'll worry you'll talk about them the same way later.
"Now's not a good time" → concession with terms
Timing objection. Like partner, it's 80% uncertainty wearing a clock costume. Double tie-down first — then if the timing constraint is real (vacation, surgery, board meeting they actually have to be in), trade a scope tweak for a decision today.
Double tie-down
"Got it. So if I took timing entirely out of the picture — would you start tonight?"

If they hesitate at the Future seed, that's uncertainty leaking out. Handle that before timing.
The deal-make if timing is real
"Here's the thing — I don't think the best thing for you is to wait and do nothing. If I'm willing to do that for you, is this something you'd move forward with right now?"
Trap: letting them "circle back in a month." Anyone who says that is 50/50 to ever return your call. Either get the deal today with a terms-traded start date, OR get an honest "no, this isn't for me right now" and move on.
Concession with terms — the universal phrasing
Across every objection — money, partner, timing, scope — when you offer a concession, you ALWAYS attach it to a decision. Never offer a concession for "let me think about it."
The format
"If I'm willing to do [X] for you, will you move forward right now?"
X = payment plan / pre-work add / start date / bonus / scope tweak. The phrase "if I'm willing to" makes it clear this is YOUR concession, not a discount they earned. "Will you move forward right now?" forces a yes/no, not a "let me think." That's the trade — and it's the only trade. Concession in exchange for a decision, never for a maybe.
Trap: announcing the concession ("we can do a 2-pay") before the trade. That's just a discount, and you've trained them to ask for more. Always lead with the conditional: "If I'm willing... will you..."

4. Quick preview: how the tool helps you run this on a call

The tool is built on top of a proven script. The script's job is to walk the prospect through every stage and get them to reveal the truth at each one, what they actually want, what's really broken, what staying stuck is costing them, who else is in the decision, whether they're really sold or just being polite. If you follow the script line-by-line, the truth comes out on its own. You almost always get to a clean yes or no.

The tool only kicks in when the prospect goes off-script, when they say something that suggests they're hiding the real answer, or hit you with an objection you didn't expect, or give a soft yes that doesn't match their tone. In those moments you type what they just said into the input box, and the copilot pops the exact next line for THAT specific moment, the loop-back probe to dig deeper, or the objection handle to diffuse and re-isolate. You'll see this properly in Tab 2. Think of it as a sales coach whispering in your ear, reading from the same script you're holding, but flagging the moment the prospect tries to slip past it.

Mental model in one sentence: the 7-stage funnel is the WHAT, the DISCOVERY mnemonic + the 5-step objection handle are the HOW, the tactics are the WHEN. You drive the call; the copilot makes sure you don't skip a step under pressure.

🎤15-minute solo practice protocol (do this BEFORE your first real call)

Reading the script doesn't put the muscle memory in. Saying it out loud does. This is a 15-minute exercise you do alone, with a mirror and a stopwatch, before your first call. It's the only way to build the nerve for the silences and the tonality on the price drop without doing them live on a real prospect.

Drill 1 (4 min), the first 60 seconds, on camera
Setup
Open your phone's selfie camera or your laptop's Photo Booth. Hit record. Stand or sit how you'd sit on a real call. Look at the lens, not the screen.
Run
Read the Frame-first opener (Example B from the script panel) out loud, looking at the camera, like a real call. Pause for 5 seconds after each sentence as if the prospect is responding. Do this 3 times.
Watch yourself back
Are your eyes looking at the camera or skittering around? Is your voice too fast? Are you smiling like a salesperson (bad) or relaxed like a doctor (good)? Notice. Re-record once.
Drill 2 (3 min), the downward-inflection price drop
Setup
Stand up. Phone in hand. You're about to do 10 reps of the same sentence.
Run
Say YOUR price drop line out loud. Force your voice DOWN on the price and DOWN on the period at the end. Exaggerate the drop. Reps 1-3 will feel weird. Reps 4-7 you'll start hearing the difference. Reps 8-10 it'll feel natural.
Example: "And Lean Body Method is twenty-four hundred dollars." [voice DROPS hard on "dollars"] "Or three payments of eight-fifty." [voice DROPS again] "Which works better for you?" [voice STAYS LOW even on the question]
Trap to avoid: letting your voice rise on the question at the end ("Which works better for you?↑"). On a real call, that turns the price into a question they can negotiate. Practice the FALLING voice on every word.
Drill 3 (5 min), THE SILENCE
Why this drill matters more than any other
After the price drop, you stop talking. The prospect responds first. If you talk first, you discount. If they talk first, they either buy or honestly object. 80% of first-time reps blow the price drop by filling the silence with "I know it's a big investment but..." or "we have payment plans available" or a nervous laugh. The silence feels like 30 seconds but is usually 5-10 seconds in reality.
Run
Stopwatch on your phone. Say the price drop line out loud. Hit start on the stopwatch the moment you stop talking. Just sit. Don't move. Don't talk. Don't fidget. Watch the stopwatch count up. Stop at 10 seconds. Note the feeling.
Do this 5 times. By rep 5, the 10 seconds will feel less unbearable. That's the muscle you need.
Drill 4 (3 min), the 5-step objection handle out loud
Run
Out loud, deliver the full 5-step sequence to an imaginary prospect who just said "let me think about it." Say each step as one continuous flow, no pauses for "what do I say next":
"No problem. Just so I understand, is it more that you're not 100% sure this is the right fit for you, or is it that you ARE sure but there's something practical in the way? ... [pretend they say money] ... Got it. So aside from the money, you feel really good about the process itself and that it would actually work for you? ... [pretend yes] ... 1 to 10, where are you? ... [pretend 8] ... What's keeping you from being a 9 or 10? ... [pretend handle] ... So if we sorted that, you'd be 100% in?"
Do this 3 times. Memorise the SHAPE (diffuse → isolate → temp check → scale → double tie-down), not the exact words. On a real call you'll improvise the connectors and the script will guide the structure.
If you do these 4 drills exactly once before your first real call, you'll have already physically practiced the moments most reps blow on their first 5 calls. That's the closest thing to coaching that exists inside this doc.

🎧 What "downward inflection" actually sounds like (since we can't put audio in a doc)
The closest thing to audio in text: read these out loud, exaggerated, to feel the difference.
WRONG (upward inflection, voice goes UP)
"And Lean Body Method is twenty-four hundred dollars?" ← your voice RISES on "dollars", like you're asking permission. Sounds nervous. Triggers their "is that negotiable?" reflex.
RIGHT (downward inflection, voice goes DOWN)
"And Lean Body Method. Is twenty-four hundred dollars." ← your voice DROPS on "dollars" like ending a statement, not a question. The period is audible. You sound certain, almost matter-of-fact. Like a doctor giving a diagnosis.

4How to use it on a real call

Read this once before you run it

This is a proven sales script. Not a chatbot. Not a "might be useful sometimes" tool. The methodology underneath has closed 8 and 9-figure offers. It works because it tells you exactly what to say, in what order, to get the prospect to be HONEST with you. Honest prospects close. Dishonest prospects turn into "let me think about it" calls that never come back.

The script does two things:

  1. Uncover the truth. What they ACTUALLY want, what's ACTUALLY stopping them, where the budget ACTUALLY lives, who's ACTUALLY in the decision. The right questions in the right order with the right tone do the digging for you.
  2. Handle every objection that comes up. 34 of them, in any order, with the exact words to say. The keyword engine watches what the prospect types and surfaces the right handle automatically.

You don't have to be a master closer to run it. You just have to read it. The script does the work. The more times you run it the more it becomes natural. Trust the script.

Three modes, in the order you actually use them:

Mode 1, BEFORE the call, run Prep first

Click ◆ Prep call in the topbar BEFORE your call. Paste your triage notes, intake form, or anything else you have on the prospect. Hit Generate prep. (Requires an Anthropic API key in Settings, sk-ant-... from console.anthropic.com. Stored only in your browser, sent only to Anthropic, costs roughly $0.02 per prep.)

You get back: what to expect, hardest DISCOVERY letters to cover with this specific prospect, dig-deeper questions tailored to them, an identity-frame line ready to use, likely objections they'll raise, and watch-outs. Read it once before you dial in. Even a 30-second skim changes how you show up.

Mode 2, DURING the call, follow the script + use HELP when stuck

You're on Zoom with the prospect. The tool is on your second screen. Use it as your script first. Click the stage you're in on the strip across the top (1. Introduction through 7. Close Confirmation) and read what the right-hand panel says, that's exactly what to say next.

When you get stuck (which will happen), three moves:

  1. Look at what's coming NEXT. The next 1-2 say-lines for this stage are right there in the script panel. Preview them so the rhythm doesn't break.
  2. Use the OPTION pickers to get the prospect to surface what they want. Where the script offers OPTION A / OPTION B (the Intro routing between Pain-first and Vision-first is the clearest example), pick the one that matches what they've said so far. The script then steers itself into the right next move.
  3. Hit the red HELP button. Type what the prospect just said into the input box, the copilot surfaces the exact objection handle or discovery probe to use right now. This is for the "they just said something weird and I don't know what to say" moments. Most reps need this in their first 5 calls, the script gets natural after that.

Also while you're on the call:

  • On the Discovery tab, click each letter chip (D · I · S · C · O · V · E · R · Y) as you cover it. Visual reminder of what's still missing.
  • Type the prospect's exact words into the input box anytime you want the copilot to scan for objections + flags. Engine catches 800+ prospect phrases automatically.
  • In Live Facts (the panel below the input), jot the prospect's exact numbers as you hear them, revenue / leads / LTV for B2B, baseline / target / timeline for transformation. The copilot uses these for the upside-math line at Committing.
📺 How to physically run this on a Zoom call without looking shifty
The tool is useless if your eyes are glued to the script and the prospect sees you reading. Here's the physical setup.
Best, two screens
Put Zoom on your laptop with the camera + your face. Put the copilot on a second monitor or iPad, raised up just below your camera. Your eyes flick down 2 inches, not sideways. From the prospect's POV you look like you're glancing at notes, not reading from a teleprompter.
One laptop, no second screen
Tile your laptop side by side: Zoom on the LEFT (next to your camera if it's on top), copilot on the RIGHT. Drag the Zoom window so the prospect's face is as close to your built-in camera as possible. When you look at the copilot, your eyes drift right, looks natural. Don't full-screen Zoom, you'll be reading sideways which looks awful.
Eye-contact protocol
Look at the camera (not the prospect's face on screen) when YOU are talking, especially during pitch + price drop. Look at the script panel when LISTENING or transitioning. The rule: connect with the camera at the moments that matter, glance at the script when they're talking. They won't notice.
Reading the script literally vs paraphrasing
For the FIRST 10 calls, read the scripts verbatim. They're written tight, you'll sound fine. After 10 calls, start swapping in your own words for the connector phrases ("are you following me" → "make sense?"). NEVER paraphrase the actual structural moves (the 5-step objection handle, the temp check, the price drop line). Those are exact for a reason, paraphrasing weakens them.
How to type / click / take notes at the same time
Don't try to type the prospect's exact words mid-sentence. Wait until they pause. Then type a SHORT version (3-5 words is enough: "money is tight" or "wants to think"). The copilot only needs enough to trigger the right card. For numbers (Live Facts), jot them down when the prospect explicitly states a number, and reference back to it when you say the upside-math line. Click DISCOVERY chips during natural pauses, not while they're speaking.
Trap to avoid: trying to drive 4 things at once on your first call. On call #1 just READ the script. On call #2 add the DISCOVERY chip strip. On call #3 add the input-box typing. Don't try to be a fighter pilot on day one.
