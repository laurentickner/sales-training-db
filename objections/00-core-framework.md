# Core Objection-Handling Framework

The connective tissue every rebuttal in this database runs through. Extracted verbatim.
The specific objection files (price, spouse, timing, etc.) all assume this skeleton.

## The 3 objection buckets

Every objection collapses into one of three: **financial**, **spouse/partner (support)**,
and **uncertainty**.

> "There's basically three big objections: there's financial, spouse/partner, and
> uncertainty... and within those three big buckets there's a ton of different little tiny
> variations." — [Cole-2Steps]

- **Uncertainty** = anything keeping them from believing this is the right thing AND now is
  the right time, *money aside*. ("I need to think about it," "send me case studies," "I've
  been burned before," "let's do next quarter," "I need to do research.")
- **Support** = they need to check with someone else (spouse, partner, board, team). NOT a
  smoke screen — it means "I 100% want to do this, I just have to check with X." A pure
  logistic.
- **Financial** = "I literally cannot make this work right now." A pure logistic.

> "Uncertainties [are] true objections. Support and financials [are] logistics. We have to
> handle the uncertainty first." — [Cole-85min]

**Why order matters:** "You can't handle financial objections or spouse objections unless
they're totally bought in on the process... If they're not certain it's gonna work, it
doesn't matter how I handle the financial objection." — [Cole-TooExpensive]

## The end-of-call process (anticipate, don't react)

> "I trained myself to really elongate [the call]... every single time I'm going to get one
> or two objections and then I'm going to close. So I expect them, I anticipate for them,
> and I prepare for them. So when they come I'm not emotionally triggered. This is part of
> the process." — [Cole-85min]

> "Don't expect to get the close when you drop the investment. Expect to get the close after
> you drop the investment, you handle three objections, and then they buy." — [Brian-podcast]

## STEP 1 — The temp check (money aside, are you certain?)

After the price drop and the first objection, do NOT chase the objection. Diffuse, then
temp-check certainty.

> "No problem. And now for a second here, let's just pretend that money is out of the
> equation. How do you feel about the process specifically? We covered four big key
> components about what we do and how we help clients get to the desired outcome — do you
> feel like ultimately those four things is what you need to grow your agency to 20k a
> month?" — [Cole-2Steps]

> "No problem. Before we get into any of that — how do you feel? You mentioned you want to
> scale your business to 100 grand a month. Do you feel like this is 100% what you need to
> be able to get there?" — [Cole-85min]

> "First, let's just answer the most important question. Do you think this is going to
> work? ... Do you think that this is what you need to get to this, and it will 100% work?"
> — [Ryder-podcast]

**"No problem" is the universal diffuser.** Use it on every objection.

> "'No problem' — it just diffuses all the sales pressure, because what they're used to
> hearing is a combative feel... if you just say 'no problem' it's diffusing the pressure."
> — [Cole-2Steps]

**Be direct. Command honesty. Use "100%."**

> "It can't be soft. It can't be passive. It has to be very direct... I'm kind of going for
> a 'no.' I really want to suss out any uncertainty they might be having on this call so I
> can address it... You must command honesty." — [Cole-85min]

## STEP 2 — Reading the answer

**A genuine YES** = fast, downward, certain cadence + big words.

> "A yes sounds like 'dude, absolutely, it's literally just funds' — fast cadence, big words
> like 'absolutely,' 'totally.' They're not bullshitting you. But if they say 'I... think
> so... no, it sounds really good' — elongated words, not that certain fast cadence — I just
> take that as a no." — [Cole-2Steps]

**Cut off wishy-washiness** — call out the tonality, playfully.

> "When you jump in and cut them off and call them out on their tonality — it's playful,
> 'hey, it doesn't sound like you're 100% certain here' — that little bit of mind reading
> builds massive trust, and you'll have leadership for the rest of the objection-handling
> phase." — [Cole-TooExpensive]

> "Got it. But what I'm really trying to understand is — do you think this is 100% going to
> work? And do you want to do this?" — [Cole-85min]

## STEP 2a — The scale of 1 to 10 (when the answer isn't an ecstatic yes)

> "I hear you, and just to be totally clear, what's really important to me is alignment.
> When you come and work with us, we're rolling up the sleeves, we're getting in the
> trenches with you — our team is all in. So what's important to us given our commitment in
> a project like this is that you feel good about the process and the work we're doing
> together. So just to be 100% clear, on a scale of one to ten — one being 'dude, I just
> hate this guy, I want to get off the phone,' and 10 being 'that sounds like exactly what I
> need' — where do you feel like you fall out exactly?" — [Cole-2Steps]

Then mine the gap:

> "Gotcha man. Well, I appreciate you being honest with me about that. Now I'm curious —
> what exactly do you think is keeping you from being like an eight, nine, or a ten?" —
> [Cole-2Steps]

> "That gap there is going to be the objection. Then we want to isolate that objection,
> handle it, and circle back around and temp-check it, re-close." — [Cole-2Steps]

## STEP 3 — The double tie-down (isolate the objection)

Once they name the objection, lock it down as the ONLY thing before handling it.

> "Money aside, you're 100% in." — [Brian-podcast] / [Cole-85min] / [Cole-TooExpensive]

> "Okay man, well just to be clear — money aside, is there anything else that's keeping you
> from being less than 100% certain that this is really what you need to be able to get to
> 20,000 a month?" — [Cole-2Steps]

> "Got it. But what I'm really trying to understand is, do you think this is 100% going to
> work? And do you want to do this?" — [Cole-85min]

Brian calls this the second, stronger tie-down:

> "That second tie-down is the key because it's very strong... I'm actually trying to get
> them to say no. If they say yes, at that point it's just logistics." — [Brian-podcast]

## The objection funnel (handle in this order)

> "There's three main objections... the most important one is uncertainty. The first thing
> you have to do is handle anything that's keeping them from being less than 100% certain
> that this is going to work. Because if they don't think it's going to work, they're not
> going to go convince their wife, they're not going to transfer money from a brokerage...
> Then if they really believe it's going to work, the three below that are just logistics —
> timing, financial, and spouse. If you handle a spouse without doing uncertainty first,
> you're handling a smoke screen." — [Brian-podcast]

## The loop

Objection handling is a loop: **temp check → isolate → handle → re-tie-down → repeat** until
they're 100% in, then assume the close or handle the logistic.

> "If they tell you an objection, you isolate it, address it, and then re-tie down. It's
> like a loop. That's why it's called looping." — [Cole-85min]
