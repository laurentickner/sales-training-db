# Objection: "I've been burned before" / "It didn't work last time"

A **solution-based uncertainty** objection: they tried something similar, it failed, now
they're skeptical. Handle the belief — they will not buy while they expect a repeat.

---

## Brian's 3-things diagnostic (force them to diagnose it your way)

> "Oh well, tell me about what happened in that company. ... What was the difference between
> what you thought was going to happen and what was actually implemented?"

> "And dude, I totally get it, because we're put in places all the time where we have to
> pick up the pieces after somebody overpromised and underdelivered. And what we found just
> doing this with thousands of clients — and it's totally just like your situation — is
> typically it comes down to one of three things. Either number one, the process they
> implemented just didn't work — they serviced you well, it's just that what they tried to
> implement strategy-wise was maybe outdated or not customized to your business. Number two,
> the strategy was good, it's just the service wasn't — it took forever, or they didn't
> support you in the right ways. Or number three, maybe both were good and you just kind of
> [ __ ] it up — maybe you just didn't show up the right way you should have. So which one
> of those three do you think it was?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** "I'm forcing them to diagnose it in a way I already know how to handle. They
  going to pick number one or number two most likely."

Then, once they pick the method:

> "Okay, what was the method? ... Can I give you a little bit of feedback on that?"
> [explain why their old method worked years ago but is weaker now / why yours is
> different] ... "So you see how our method is entirely different? So wouldn't it make sense,
> given the service quality is the same, if the method is right, that you wouldn't get the
> same results as before? So with that out of the way — do you really think this is going to
> work?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

## Cole's solution-based handle (apples-to-oranges comparison)

Prospect: *"I like everything you said on paper, but you never know until you actually do
it. We tried somebody else in the past that was similar and it didn't work."*

> "Totally get it. And yeah, that's super frustrating — a lot of times we're put in the
> place where we have to pick up the pieces of somebody overpromising and then deliver. So
> just so I can be clear, is there anything about what I outlined specifically that's
> keeping you from being less than 100% certain that this is going to work? Or is it purely
> a hesitation based on that bad experience that's just really giving you pause?"

> "Great. So outside of that, you're essentially 100% in."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Note:** Sometimes they add a second objection here ("I also have to figure out the
  money"). Handle the uncertainty FIRST, then the money.

Then the apples-to-oranges discovery:

> "Well, how many clients did this [provider] have? What was their revenue? Did they
> actually have a sales team, or was it just a one-man shop and you talked to the founder?
> Have they worked with any top names? What were their case studies like? How long have
> they been around?"

> "So you're comparing somebody who has three employees and probably five clients with no
> sales team, doing all the sales themselves, basically no credibility, very little case
> studies — with somebody who's been the category king of the space, has done this for six
> years, worked with all the top brands in the industry, has almost 3,000-plus clients and
> all these Trustpilot reviews, and most importantly actually has a sales team doing eight
> figures, which is exactly what you want. How does that comparison make any sense? So I'm
> sorry that you did this in the past and it didn't work, but you learned the lesson of
> 'don't work with a beginner' — don't learn the wrong lesson there and make that lesson
> 'not doing something that's right.' You've got to invest. This is a part of business."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

## Burned-specific lines from Brian's "think-about-it" roleplay

(Full sequence is in `think-about-it.md`; these are the burned-before-specific lines,
duplicated here on purpose.)

> "What happened when you got burnt? What was the situation there?"

> "I'm not saying I'm going to guarantee you the outcomes are going to happen, because that
> would be naive — we are in a market that has conditions. But there is a bottom rung to
> that, which is you should 100% feel sure that the deliverables and what we're going to do
> on our side are going to happen."

> "Can I level with you on a thought? The deliverables is easy — it's going to be in the
> contract. You'll have it contractually guaranteed that we're going to do the deliverables.
> A company like ours doesn't get to work with people like Tony Robbins, Dean Graziosi,
> Grant Cardone if we don't deliver. You would know if we're not delivering, because you
> would see it everywhere. So it's pretty easy for us to make sure the deliverables happen.
> But I also want to make sure that you actually feel good about working with us, because a
> big part of those outcomes happening or not is whether you're ready to get over the past
> burn and fully commit with us."

> "Do you want to let the past burn stop you from making change in the future? ... No. I
> don't think you do either."

> "Well dude, let's just go ahead and get everything signed and get going, cuz man — bro to
> bro — if you let the past burn inform who you're going to be in the future and the
> decisions you're going to make, you're just going to stay stuck, and you know that. It
> doesn't change that it's hard, but I don't think you want to let past burn stop you from
> achieving things in the future."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

## ANTI-PATTERN — do not do this

> "What's the terrible closer say? 'Oh well, have you ever dated before? ... Yeah, well, if
> your girlfriend broke up with you, would you never date again?' You don't want to do
> that." — [Brian-podcast]

Don't argue them out of the feeling with a glib analogy. Diagnose what actually happened,
then separate it from your offer.
