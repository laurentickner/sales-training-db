# Objection: Timing — "I want to start later" / "After the holidays" / "Next quarter"

A timing/uncertainty objection. The move: change the terms so the delay becomes irrelevant —
let them start the logistics now, push the real start date out. Then it's buy, or the real
objection surfaces.

---

## The "start after the holidays" handle (add days to the engagement)

Prospect: *"This sounds great. I want to start January 3rd."*

> "Got it. So timing aside, you're 100% in? ... So just to be clear, if we're having this
> same exact conversation on January 3rd and you're feeling the same exact way you feel
> right now, then you're 100% giving me your credit card and moving forward?"

> "Well, I've got an idea. Are you open to it? ... Cool. Well look, I don't think — based on
> what we talked about on this call and X, Y, and Z and what you brought up about your
> current situation — that the best thing for you is to wait and do nothing. But I also
> don't think the best thing is to hop in right now if you're going on vacation, you're
> doing this and that, and you're not going to have the full time to be able to commit to
> this. So what I'd be willing to do for you is add 30 days onto your engagement with us.
> That way you could start now, knock out the onboarding, get some of the forms done, get
> some of these initial items out of the way — then when your official start date comes
> around January 3rd, we can really hit the ground running. So if I was willing to do that
> for you, would you be willing to move forward right now?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** Same "I don't think / I also don't think" structure as the payment-plan
  close. Scale the added days to the delay — 7 days, 2 weeks, 30 days, up to ~45. Run this
  starting November 1 for January-start objections — and note the prospect wouldn't really
  be engaged over Thanksgiving / December anyway, so you're barely giving up any time.

> "What we did here is they had an objection or a logistic — we changed the terms now to
> make that irrelevant. So they either have to buy, or it prompts the conversation of what's
> really going on." — [Cole-85min]

## "Move money around" — take a down payment now

Prospect: *"I have to move money around, then I'm 100% in. Give me till Friday."*

> "Okay, so just walk me through it real fast so I can stay organized on my end. When you
> say you're moving money around, what does that look like? Where is it coming from? Are you
> selling off some stocks? Are you taking it out of a 401k or a brokerage account? Walk me
> through that. ... And when is that going to be finished by? ... And then when that's
> finished, you're 100% moving forward?"

> "Let's do seven and a half now — that way we can still get you onboarding, get the
> assessment, get the forms, get the onboarding call out of the way — and then we'll just
> take care of the rest in a week when you're done moving money around."

> "Look, let's get this done now. That way you get access to A, B, C, you can get your
> onboarding forms out of the way — but just make me a gentleman's agreement: you're going
> to get up to half down before you do your onboarding call."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** "Grease in the chute" — take one or two payments now, get them up to the
  3-pay (or onboarding) threshold within a week or two. (Also filed in
  `price-and-financial.md`.)

## Matt Ryder's "do it later" reframe

> "Is the way that we put it together make sense? Do you feel like that would work? Okay,
> cool. So when do you want to start actually doing the thing that you know will work? Do
> you want to do it later? You could do it later. I've seen less successful people do it
> that way."

- **Source:** [Ryder-podcast]
- **Context:** A light, cheeky reframe — said with humor, not pressure. Only works once
  they've confirmed the method will work.

---

## ANTI-PATTERN — do not do this

> "Average salespeople just accept this and then wait two days, book the follow-up... but
> even if they genuinely are 100% in, they get off the call and they're like 'yeah, maybe
> 50% in,' and they don't show up. There's desire fatigue." — [Cole-85min]

Don't book a passive follow-up and hope. Slow it down, understand the actual logistic, and
take a down payment now so the deal is real.
