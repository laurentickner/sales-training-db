# Objection: "I want to think about it" / "Sleep on it" / "Send me a proposal"

Covers "I need to think about it," "send me the info in an email," "I need to do my
research," "I want to read reviews / talk to your clients," "I don't make decisions on the
first call." This is an **uncertainty** objection — handle it before any logistic.

---

## Open the conversation (diffuse, then dig)

> "Okay, that's totally fine. While we're here, are you just open to talking through what's
> going through your head? Can you help me understand kind of what's happening?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

> "Can I ask you a personal question? ... How are you feeling about it? ... What about it is
> feeling good right now? What are the parts that are feeling good?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Get them naming what they *like* first — builds positive momentum before
  surfacing the real hesitation.

---

## The temp check (is it a smoke screen?)

> "No problem. Before we get into any of that — how do you feel? You mentioned you want to
> scale your business to 100 grand a month. Do you feel like this is 100% what you need to
> be able to get there?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

> "No problem. So just so I could be clear, how do you feel in terms of getting your
> business to 100 grand a month? Do you feel like this is 100% what you need to be able to
> get there, and this is 100% going to work?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

> "No problem. And now for a second, let's just pretend money is out of the equation. How do
> you feel about the process specifically? Do you feel like ultimately those four things is
> what you need to grow your agency to 20k a month?"

- **Source:** [Cole-2Steps]

Brian's double tie-down version:

> "Hey, no problem. That's totally cool. Let me ask you this, just because we've been
> through a lot today — I covered step one, step two, step three that we'd be implementing
> together. Do you feel like money and everything aside, that is what you need to get to
> that goal? ... So money and everything aside, you 100% believe this will get you to XYZ
> revenue."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** "That second tie-down is the key... I'm actually trying to get them to say
  no" — so any hidden uncertainty surfaces now.

If they waffle:

> "Got it. But what I'm really trying to understand is — do you think this is 100% going to
> work? And do you want to do this?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

---

## Isolating the "sleep on it"

> "No issue at all. I'm totally good with you sleeping on it. And honestly, based on your
> experience in the past, I would sleep on it too if I was you. So just to clarify, if we
> schedule a time tomorrow and you wake up feeling tomorrow the same exact way you feel
> right now, then what you're telling me is we're 100% processing the payment and moving
> forward."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** The phrase "if we schedule a time *tomorrow*" pre-supposes a next-day
  follow-up and anchors the timeline tight — don't let the default become "same time next
  week." Desire fatigues.

24-hour-rule variation:

> "No, I 100% want to do it, I literally just have a personal rule where I have to wait 24
> hours before I ever make a decision." → "Gotcha man, so just to be clear, you're just
> doing a gut check and taking 24 hours to make sure you're waking up tomorrow feeling the
> same way you feel right now — that aside, are you 100% in? Is there anything else that's
> keeping you feeling less than 100% that this is really what you need? ... Gotcha, so if
> you wake up tomorrow and you feel the same way you feel right now, we're 100% moving
> forward."

- **Source:** [Cole-2Steps]

---

## The "come in now, refund if you hate it" option

> "Look, why don't you come in now? Let's draw the line in the sand and make a commitment
> today. And then if you wake up tomorrow feeling like 'oh my gosh, this is the worst thing
> in the world,' and at the bottom of the pit of my stomach I know I made a terrible
> decision — we'll just refund you."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** For prospects who are right there. Don't use it on visibly sensitive
  "sleep-on-it" personalities — just let those sleep on it, but lock the next-day call.

---

## Diffusing the follow-up call (so they actually show up)

By default, the only reason a prospect shows up to a follow-up is to say yes — so if they
feel "no" or "not yet" that day, they ghost. Proactively remove the pressure:

> "Awesome, let's get that time booked for tomorrow. ... Hey, so one last thing tomorrow — I
> want to go over two specific case studies of clients in your exact industry, exactly where
> we started with them, what we implemented, what their ROI was and what time frames. Plus
> I'll talk about some payment options that'll maybe make this easier for you to break it
> up, that I just got to check with management with first, but I think we'll be fine for
> you. So I've got a lot to cover with you — we really haven't even covered everything. So
> don't feel like you have to come to that next call with any sort of decision. All I want
> you to do is come to the call with questions... Let's cover the questions along with the
> information that I have, and then once you have all the information, we can figure out
> whatever decision for you is the best decision — whether that's a now thing or it's all
> good with me if we have to build towards that in the future. Does that sound good for
> tomorrow?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** Open loops (case studies, payment options) give the prospect a reason to
  show up beyond saying yes. Their #1 best closer uses this and runs a 90%+ follow-up show
  rate.
