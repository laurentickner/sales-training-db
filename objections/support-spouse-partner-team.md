# Objection: Support — "I need to talk to my spouse / partner / board / team"

A **support** objection: they need to check with someone else (up, down, or sideways —
spouse, business partner, board, team). Treated as a *logistic*, NOT a smoke screen — but
ONLY once they're 100% sold themselves. Handle uncertainty first.

> "What a lot of closers will do is they will just chase this — so it's actually a smoke
> screen — then they start going through the spousal objection framework and the person's
> not even sold." — [Cole-85min]

**Goal on the call:** get the person in front of you 100% sold (emotionally, viscerally) so
they become your champion. "If they're not 100% sold, they're not going to convince
somebody we've never even spoken with."

---

## Proactively surface decision-makers (before the objection ever lands)

> "Quick question before we go further. Besides you, who else needs to be comfortable with
> this if we decide this is the right move?"

- **Source:** [Abuvala-$30M]
- **Context:** Asked early, right after setting the frame. Phrased "who else needs to be
  comfortable" so it also pre-handles the spouse objection. Branches the call: all
  decision-makers present → main script; not present → Champion Script.

Marketing-side gate (application):

> "We need both the parents and the students on this call, and they can't book the call
> unless they confirm that everybody's going to show up."

- **Source:** [Abuvala-$30M]

The tie-down later, if they stall:

> "Well, didn't you tell me earlier that you were the only person that needs to be
> comfortable with this?"

- **Source:** [Abuvala-$30M]
- **Context:** Turns a lot of "I need to run it by my partner" stalls into closes — not a
  guaranteed close, but it converts hesitators.

**The Champion Script concept:** if the decision-maker isn't on the call, don't re-book and
lose them — "give this person who's on the call enough assets and information that they can
take this to their decision maker and then bring us all on a call together." — [Abuvala-$30M]

## The temp check (are YOU sold?)

> "No problem. Okay, just so I can be clear — how do you feel for you personally? Do you
> 100% want to do this?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

> "Cool. Just to clarify, if it was solely your decision, we'd be processing the payment and
> you'd be 100% moving forward."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** The double tie-down for support objections — definitive language.

> "Gotcha, so just to be clear — really getting your wife bought into this aside, is there
> anything else that's keeping you from personally being less than 100% certain that this is
> really what you need?"

- **Source:** [Cole-2Steps]

## Spouse roleplay — full sequence (refundable deposit + onboard the spouse)

Prospect: *"Well, it sounds really good, I love it, I just want to talk to my wife."*

> "Is she home? ... Will you go grab her? ... Can we call her right now?"

> "Can I ask you a personal question? Is this like a 'man, she's going to divorce me if I
> say yes and kill me' thing, or is it like you two wanting to make sure that you're on the
> same page and she respects the —"

> "Have you two talked through this up to this point? ... Is she your business partner? ...
> So she's not involved in business operations. Is she like a business consultant — keeps
> you on the rails, makes sure you don't do anything dumb?"

> "Help me understand what kind of — what you're looking for in her approval. And I know
> that sounds like an [ __ ] question, but if she's not a business partner, she's not a
> consultant, I'm just trying to better understand —"

> "If you move forward without talking to her, what would happen? ... Have you messed that
> one up before, bro? ... In what way?"

> "Are you open to a middle ground solution? ... Before I present it, shoot it with me
> straight — do you actually want to do this, or —"

> "All right, dude, there's an easy solution to this. Here's all we're going to do. We're
> going to put down a $2,000 deposit now. It's a refundable deposit. And then what I want to
> do is schedule a call with you and your wife and me, so that she can meet me, meet the
> company, and I'll actually onboard you into the curriculum so that she can see what it is
> that you invested into. Because if we don't do that, I'm just another guy pitching you
> another thing. It's going to depend on where she's at on the day and where her mood's at.
> I'd rather her be able to actually see the thing that you're potentially investing into.
> So let's do the deposit now, I'll onboard you into the curriculum, and then us three can
> jump on later tonight."

> "If she actually hates our guts and thinks the onboarding is the worst thing ever and is
> wondering why the heck you did this, we'll just send it back. But if she feels really good
> like you do, then you're already well on your way... You told me earlier the most
> important thing for you is to not let the last 5 years be like the next 5 years. So let's
> put down this refundable deposit. There's no risk in doing it, but for you that's
> metaphorically drawing the line in the sand and saying that from here on out things are
> going to be different. So if I was willing to make that adjustment to how we regularly do
> things for you, would you be willing to move forward right now?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** The recap principle: "do you need their permission, or are you just letting
  them know? ... especially with a guy, you can just go to the refundable deposit — book
  the onboarding call with you and your wife, and she can get onboarded with you so she's
  just as bought in as you are."

## Partner / board roleplay — let-them-know vs. collaboration

Prospect: *"This sounds great, can you send this over? I need to sync with my partner on it
and get back to you."*

> "No problem. Okay, just so I can be clear, how do you feel for you personally? Do you 100%
> want to do this?" → "Cool, just to clarify, if it was solely your decision, we'd be
> processing the payment and you'd be 100% moving forward."

> "Perfect. Well, I'll make sure to send everything over. Now is this something to where you
> basically made the decision and you're just going to let them know, or is this something
> you guys need to really collaborate on?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Rule of thumb:** "Anything but a definitive 'I'm definitely doing this, I just have to
  let them know' is a collaboration."

Then go back into discovery on the absent decision-maker:

> "Okay, well cool — what do you think they're going to think? What do you think they're
> going to say about the price? ... What would be the reason, if anything, they wouldn't
> want to do this?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** Figure out: is it a let-them-know or collaboration? Any landmines with the
  partner? Do you trust this person to sell the partner, do you need the partner ON the
  call, or do you need to pep-talk your champion through the conversation?
