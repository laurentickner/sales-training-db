# Objection: Price / Financial — "It's too expensive" / "I can't afford it"

Covers "that's too much," "I can't afford it," "it's more than I thought," and the true
financial logistic ("I want to do it, I just have to find the money"). For "your competitor
is cheaper" see `competitor-cheaper.md`. For "I have to move money around / start later" the
deal-making move is at the bottom of this file and in `timing-start-later.md`.

**Rule before you start:** First isolate whether this is a true financial objection or a
smoke screen for uncertainty. Money cannot be handled until they're 100% bought in. See
`00-core-framework.md`.

---

## Isolating: is it real, or a smoke screen?

> "No problem. Now just to stay organized on my end — when you say you can't afford it, are
> you more in a 'should I do this' place to where you're not even 100% certain this is
> really what you want to do, or kind of more in a 'how can I do this' place to where you're
> 100% certain that you want to do this, it's just a matter of making it work financially?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]
- **Context:** Said immediately after the prospect gives the price objection, before any
  payment-plan talk. Binary options on purpose — objection-handling shuts off the creative
  brain, so give them an either/or.

> "Gotcha. So if I'm hearing you correctly, money aside, you're 100% in?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]
- **Context:** The double tie-down. Listen for fast, certain tonality. "If they say 'I
  think so... no'... I'll cut them off and be like, 'okay, let's just be honest, it doesn't
  sound like you're 100% certain.'"

> "Hey, no problem. Totally get it. Outside of the money — and this is what we covered
> earlier — do you feel like those three things we covered, the three things we'd be
> implementing together, is exactly what you need to be able to get to X, Y, and Z outcome?
> ... So money aside, you're 100% in."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Brian's financial framework. The prospect must say it "with certainty, not
  the waffling, not 'I think so.' If they say 'I think so' — 'what's really going on?'"

> "And that's okay. I probably didn't explain everything the right way. But tell me what
> specifically is keeping you from being 100% certain that this is what you need — because I
> have people like you doing X, Y, and Z, doing way bigger numbers, and this is what they
> did. So I know it works. But for it to work, you have to believe it works. So what's
> specifically holding you back?"

- **Source:** [Ryder-podcast]
- **Context:** When the prospect goes silent / "ahh" after "do you think this will work?"

## Getting permission to talk payment plans

Payment plans are never offered up front. Once it's confirmed a true financial logistic,
get permission to open the wallet.

> "Gotcha. Well look, most of my clients do the investment up front, but for certain clients
> in situations where they really want to do it, it's just the money — we break it up. So
> given you're 100% in, and it's really just a way we can make this work financially, are
> you open to exploring the possibility of that?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]

> "Well, it depends. Everything is really all customized and predicated on what you need. So
> are you open to having an honest conversation financially, getting everything on the
> table, so we can see if there's a possibility of doing this now — or at the very least we
> can create a game plan to work towards in the future?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]
- **Context:** The "now or at the very least a game plan for the future" framing removes
  the risk of them feeling they'll be high-pressure closed for sharing their numbers.

> "Most of our clients do it upfront, but for certain clients who really want to do it like
> you, but finances are standing in the way, we break it up. So are you open to having an
> honest conversation financially, getting everything on the table so we can see if there's
> a way to make this work now — or at the very least, we can create a game plan for you to
> work towards this in the future?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

> "So look, most of our clients do it up front, but for certain clients, depending on the
> situation, we break it up. So are you open to having an honest conversation financially,
> getting everything out on the table, so we can see if there's a way we can make this work
> now or at the very least we can create a game plan together to make this work in the
> future?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

## The open wallet (collecting cash flow → cash on hand → credit)

Ask cash flow FIRST (least invasive), then stair-step into cash on hand and credit.

> "Got it. So in the next 30 days, how much cash flow do you have coming in total? ... And
> how much do you have left over after expenses, after paying the bills? ... Gotcha, cool.
> And what's your cash on hand exactly right now? ... Is that checking or savings? ... And
> what's in the checking? ... And that's the exact number you have available? ... And in
> terms of credit — not saying we have to use it, but just so we know what's available and
> kind of the state of the union here — how many cards do you have currently, what's the
> availability, how much of that is used right now?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]

> "So first, what's net incoming cash in the next 30 days — after what you have coming in,
> expenses, what's going to be left in 30 days? ... Gotcha, and what's your cash on hand
> right now exactly? ... Well, I'm not asking what you can spend right now, and I don't even
> know if you can do this right now, but I'm asking what do you actually have access to? So
> what's in checking? ... [then] credit."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

> "What's your net incoming 30-day cash? ... And what's your cash on hand right now exactly?
> ... Well, I'm not asking what you can spend right this second. I'm just asking how much
> you actually have access to right now, just so we have the landscape of what's available.
> ... And then what about credit?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Brian asks the easy one (30-day cash) first "to get them in the pattern of
  answering a question," then the harder two.

**Handling resistance to sharing numbers:**

> "Look, I'm not asking how much you can spend right now, I'm just asking what you have
> available so we can see if we can make this work — and regardless if it's a possibility
> now or later on, at the very least I can leave you with a game plan to work towards in the
> future."

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]
- **Note:** If they lie or give a non-exact number ("uh, 500 bucks"), it was never a
  financial objection — it's a smoke screen for uncertainty. Go back and handle that.

**When they discover it's bad — cut them off before they spiral:**

> "John, it's worse than you thought, isn't it? ... Look, let's just see what it is. I don't
> even know if you can do this, but we need to have an honest conversation, because the
> worst thing I could do is not have clarity on that and then leave you in a place where you
> have nothing to work towards in the future."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

> "Dude, it sounds like we need to get making some more money."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Teased lightly when the prospect realizes their finances are thin. Keeps the
  pressure pot diffused with humor.

## Re-anchor desire before presenting the plan

> "Gotcha. And you really want to do this, right?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive] / [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Said after the open wallet (which "pushes them away" a little) to pull them
  back in before presenting terms.

## Presenting the payment plan (the "I don't think / I also don't think" close)

> "Okay, because look — I don't think the best thing for you is to drop 12k right now and
> spend literally all of your savings and go into credit card debt. But I also — based on
> what you told me earlier that the business is the most important thing in your life and if
> you don't do something you're going to have to go back to corporate within the next six to
> twelve months — because of that, I also don't think the best thing for you is to do
> nothing. And besides, for you it's what, two clients at 6k to break even, right? ... So
> because of that, what I'd be willing to do for you is let you in for a third down — so 4k
> right now — and then that way you can come in, build out your offer, build your lead gen,
> close a few clients, and that way you can bankroll the rest of the payments so when that
> second and third payment comes around 30, 60 days down the road, those payments are
> basically just an afterthought. Is that fair? So if that's something I'm willing to do for
> you, is that something you're willing to move forward with right now?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]

> "Well, look, in your situation, I don't think doing this all up front — I wouldn't even
> recommend or even allow you to do that. But I also don't think the best thing for you to
> do based on what we talked about earlier is to do nothing. So what I'd be willing to do
> for you is let you in for half down. That way you can implement X, implement Y, implement
> Z, to where when that next payment comes around 30 days down the road, you already have A,
> B, and C built out, you have tons of momentum, to where it's going to feel almost like an
> afterthought. So if I was willing to do that for you, are you willing to move forward
> right now?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

> "Well, because — good, because look, I don't think the best thing for you right now is to
> do this all up front if it's going to zero you out or if it's going to be half of your net
> worth. I also don't think the best thing for you to do based on the conversation we had is
> to do nothing. So what I'd be willing to do for you is, if we split it into two to where
> you could do half now and half in 30 days — if I'm willing to do that for you, will you
> move forward right now?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

> "Because look, I don't think the best thing for you is to drop 12k right now and spend all
> your savings and go into credit card debt. I also don't think the best thing for you is to
> do nothing. And besides — for you it's what, two clients at 6k to break even, right? So
> that's basically your downside. And your upside, if you come in, you do the work, you
> raise your hand when you need help, is we hit 30k a month in profit, which is the number
> you want to hit."

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]
- **Context:** The downside/upside ROI math. Frame the downside as the tiny number of
  sales needed just to break even.

## Trade the concession for a decision (never just give the plan away)

> "If I'm willing to do that for you, are you willing to move forward right now?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast] / [Cole-85min] / [Cole-TooExpensive]
- **Context:** Any concession (pay-in-full → 2-pay → 3-pay) must be traded for something —
  most often a decision now. "Anytime you're making a concession, you want to ask for
  something in return."

> "Never let them do 'How about a 2-pay?' What you're doing here is you're making a
> concession with terms in exchange for a decision... If you just say 'no problem, we'll
> break it into two,' then breaking it into two has no value." — [Cole-85min]

If you have to go lower (down to a 3-pay), ask for a decision now **and** a case study:

> "If I'm going to do that for you, you also need to give me a case study." — [Brian-podcast]

## When they still resist after the plan

> "Hey, what's really going on? Because you can definitely do this. You said you really
> wanted to do it, but now given an opportunity to do it, we're hitting some resistance here
> and we're not moving forward. So just be honest with me — what's really going on?"

- **Source:** [Cole-TooExpensive]
- **Context:** Once the open wallet proves they CAN afford the plan, any remaining
  resistance is fear/uncertainty — see `nerves-cant-pull-trigger.md`.

## "Move money around" — deal-making (down payment now)

> "Okay, so just walk me through it real fast so I can stay organized on my end. When you
> say you're moving money around, what does that look like? Where is it coming from? Are you
> selling off some stocks? Are you taking it out of a 401k or a brokerage account? Walk me
> through that. ... And when is that going to be finished by? ... And then when that's
> finished, you're 100% moving forward?"

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

> "Let's do seven and a half now — that way we can still get you onboarding, get the
> assessment, get the forms, get the onboarding call out of the way — and then we'll just
> take care of the rest in a week when you're done moving money around."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]

> "Look, let's get this done now. That way you get access to A, B, C, you can get your
> onboarding forms out of the way — but just make me a gentleman's agreement: you're going
> to get up to half down before you do your onboarding call."

- **Source:** [Cole-85min]
- **Context:** "Grease in the chute" deals — take one or two payments now, get them up to
  the 3-pay threshold within a week or two.

---

## ANTI-PATTERN — do not do this

> "Giving a payment plan is not effective objection handling. It's pathetic. The prospect
> says 'I want to think about it' and the salesperson is like 'yeah, well, what if we just
> do four payments?' ... Giving better payments is not overcoming uncertainty." — [Cole-85min]

Lowering price / better terms does not fix uncertainty. Handle the belief first.
