# Objection: Nerves / Fear — "I just can't pull the trigger"

When every logistic is handled, they've said they're 100% in, and they STILL freeze. Not a
stated objection — a felt one. You name it for them, then make it about identity.

---

## Release the pressure first — "what's really going on?"

> "Man, can I ask you a personal question? There's probably something else going through
> your head. I'm not trying to prod at you, but we've been talking for an hour — from my
> end, it seems like everything about this makes sense for you. What else is going on? Will
> you just open up and talk to me about what you're thinking?"

> "Hey man, well, just level with me — what's really going on? Earlier you said you wanted
> to do this, you said it sounds really good. Look, if you do it now or do it later, that's
> fine. But to me, it feels like something is on your mind that we just haven't discussed
> yet. And if we're going to work together in the future, I want to make sure we're fully
> aligned — and to do that, we just have to be honest with each other. So what's really
> going on?"

> "Dude, your business is in such a good spot to grow right now. Seems like everything makes
> sense, but maybe I missed something. What's going through your head?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Stay neutral and playful. "If you can stay neutral and playful at the close,
  people will just be honest. If you tense up, they put their guards up." Ask gently a few
  times; if they still won't communicate, you did everything you could.

---

## Name the nerves (the "nervousness close")

> "It just kind of seems like it's a little bit of nerves."

> "Look, the nerves are good. That means there's some weight to this — there's some meaning
> to it. Nothing ever great in the world was built by somebody who wasn't a little bit
> nervous at first. So the nerves are really relevant in this conversation. The real
> conversation is — who do you want to be? Do you want to be the type of person who can feel
> the fear and do it anyways, or the type of person who lets that nervousness dictate their
> decision-making? Which is it?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Give them the objection — "is it a little bit of nerves?" — don't say "is it
  fear?" ("It's not fear. Are you just scared? It's not fear.") "Nerves" they'll accept.

---

## The head-vs-heart tug-of-war close

> "Can I ask you a weird question? Does it feel like your head and heart are like playing
> tug-of-war? I'm only asking because I've been through this — where your heart is like 'I
> know I want to do this,' but your mind and your body is like 'don't [ __ ] do it.' And it
> feels like you're playing tug-of-war."

> "Yeah, it's heavy, huh? ... Well, which one do you think we should listen to — your heart,
> or your body that's freaking out? Which one do you think is going to take you farther?"

> "It's just an internal tug-of-war. Your brain's trying to keep you safe. Your brain's
> yelling at you to not do it. You hear your mom's voice in the back of your head saying
> you're going to be a failure and this is bad. But your heart knows. Your heart is the
> thing that lit up when earlier you told me what it would mean to make 10 grand a month —
> that was your heart lighting up. And now your heart is whispering at you, 'Hey, you know
> you need to do this.' But every adult you've ever interacted with in your head right now
> is like 'Nope, you're a loser.' So — which one?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast] (host's line)

## Match the approach to the person — supportive vs. push

> "I'm either going to be supporting someone in making a decision, or pushing them. Once I
> know it's fear, I delineate: is this a prideful person, or a person who's just fearful? If
> they're prideful / arrogant, I'll have to push them. If they're just scared, they need to
> feel supported." — [Brian-podcast]

### Supportive frame (the scared prospect — e.g. a stressed single mom)

> "Hey, can I ask you a personal question? And this is going to sound tough. If we don't
> make some sort of change — hearing you talk about your schedule right now, your kids — do
> you really want to be stuck in this for the next six months? ... And this is extremely
> scary for you, I see that. This is a huge decision. ... I want you to know I see that for
> you, and I'm on your team. Anything I say — this is going to be a big scary decision in
> this moment, and that's not going to go away. But I need you to know we're going to hold
> your hand through this process and we have your back. I'm going to text you from my
> personal cell phone. You're going to meet with coach so-and-so. I so deeply see the
> situation that you're in, and I know it's not easy. And when I look at what you want to do
> for your kids and for your life, I just want to help you get there. Will you trust us to
> help you start moving down that path? Will you please let us help?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

### Push frame (the prideful prospect)

> "Dude, can I shoot it with you straight? ... This is going to piss you off, is that okay?
> ... Dude, you've been stuck for 6 years. [pause] And dude, I'm not trying to be an ass —
> but unless we acknowledge the reality of what's going on, we're not going to be able to
> change it. Earlier you told me you want to get to 300 grand a year. If you're just
> leveling with me — is that going to happen if you're staying where you're at? ... So I
> guess the real question, dude — and again this feels pointed, but it is the real question
> — is that goal actually real and meaningful to you, or is it just a thing that we're
> talking about? ... Well then, dude, you know what the right things to do are. So are you
> going to do the right things — yes or no?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

## Why this works — humor releases pressure

> "The end of a sales call is like a pressure pot with steam-release valves. Elite closers
> use humor to release the pressure at the right time and in the right way." — [Brian-podcast]

> "When the fun meter goes up, the pressure meter can also go up — and you can get a lot
> more decisions quickly." — [Ryder-podcast]
