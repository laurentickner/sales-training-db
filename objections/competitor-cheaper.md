# Objection: "Your competitor is cheaper" / "It's more than I thought"

The prospect is shopping you against another option (usually cheaper). Reframe the decision
from price to *who is most likely to get them the outcome*. Educate — never bulldoze the
competitor.

---

## Brian's full roleplay — "way more than I thought / others are cheaper"

Prospect: *"Yeah, it sounds good, but it's way more than I thought it was going to be. I
looked into some other things. Usually this runs about 10K. Yours was 15."*

> "Yeah man, I'm curious — how are you thinking about it? Help me understand where you —"

> "What held you back from going with the other ones, I'm curious?"

> "Well, I guess ultimately — how would you know if you did find the right team to help
> you?" → [prospect: "I'd feel very confident they could get me where I want to go"] → "So
> it's actually really just about who's going to get you to where you want to go. Is there
> anything else?"

> "Do you want to work with a team that has impeccable industry credibility? ... Well man,
> what about our conversation led you to feel like we weren't the people that could help you
> get there?"

> "Well, is it about the cheaper thing, or is it about working with the team that's going to
> help you get there, bro? ... Are you sure? Cuz dude, there's people that are cheaper than
> us — I promise I can refer you to them, brother. I'm happy to."

> "But dude, can I just be your bro for a sec? I don't think you need the cheapest thing
> right now. You're doing a hundred grand a month. You want to get to $500,000 a month.
> Whether the investment's five grand or 50 grand relative to that goal — who gives a
> [ __ ]? Even if I pitched you 30 grand and you got to 500, would you really look back and
> care at all?"

> "Can I ask — the other companies you've talked to, have they done the thing that you want
> to do? ... Do you know to what scale? ... Because that's a big part of it, man. There's a
> lot of people that have done it a few times. There's very few people who have done it
> hundreds of times, if not thousands. And to level with you — they also might be good. I'm
> not saying you shouldn't work with them. I think you should go with the team that's done
> what you want to do the most amount of times, and that you're the most sure can help you
> get there. That might be us, that might be them."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

## Brian's "price aside, who are you more certain about" reframe

> "Hey, great. So aside from evaluating which option you're going to go with, it sounds like
> you're going to do something — it's just a matter of who, right? So let's just take price
> aside for a moment. Out of the two options, just which are you more certain between us and
> this other company? ... Between us and ABC — just based on what we've talked about in this
> conversation, price and everything aside — which is more likely to be able to get you
> there?"

> "And I totally get that, cuz I've never even heard of that company, and we've been doing
> this for X amount of years, we've worked with thousands of clients. So obviously we're
> going to charge a little bit more. So the real question isn't 'are we more versus them.'
> You told me you wanted to get to $400,000 a month in your business. And you also told me
> you think we're much more likely to get you there than this other person who's just 5K
> cheaper. So — do you want to nickel and dime and go with the lesser option and jeopardize
> the success that you want, and then eventually come back later anyways? Or do you just
> want to go with us and get it right from the start? Because if we get you from 100 to 400,
> what's 5K?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

## Educate, don't bulldoze

> "Where most closers mess up is they're always trying to prop us up and tear them down. ...
> When you diminish the other thing, what's in their brain? 'Oh, they're a closer, they're
> just going to say whatever they've got to say.'" — [Brian-podcast]

Instead, educate on the options and sequence them:

> "Low ticket's great. Now, what I would say for you guys is, from my experience working
> with thousands of clients and seeing companies just like yours get to 800,000 a month —
> the way they do it is they go from one to about four to five-six-hundred with a call
> funnel, they get their messaging right, their setters right, their sales team right. So
> it's not that you shouldn't do that — but that's more for when you get up to here. You've
> got to make the decisions in proper sequence. So I think that's a great idea — I'd just do
> it here for you guys, not here. And honestly, we're really great at low-ticket funnels —
> if I thought that's what you needed now, I just would have pitched you that."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

The "you're limiting yourself" reframe (when they want to compare two specialists):

> "Man, you guys are limiting yourself. ... You're doing 100 grand a month and you're 25.
> You thinking that it's 'working with us OR this other guy who's brilliant at marketing' is
> silly. He's going to help you with your marketing, and nobody else has done what we do at
> the rate you want to do it. You have to think through this in order of priority. So in
> order of priority — come work with us, and then in three months go work with that guy
> also. You guys are just being silly."

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]

Refusing the "give us a couple days" follow-up:

> [Prospect: "Can you just give us a couple days?"] "Nope, I'm not doing that. ... Cuz
> that's silly. We're all here together — why could we not just make the decision?"

- **Source:** [Brian-podcast]
- **Context:** Stay chill and playful, not tense. "You messaged me on Instagram yesterday —
  so you're smart. I'm not following up with you in 3 days. Let's just decide right now."

## The Ravi-side framing — make them do the ROI math

While not delivered as a competitor rebuttal, the "conservative upside math" reframe makes
price irrelevant against any cheaper option:

> "Quick math so this isn't abstract. Your average client is worth [LTV] in lifetime value.
> If the system just adds one more client a month over the next 12 months, that's [LTV × 12]
> in additional revenue, conservatively speaking."

- **Source:** [Abuvala-$30M]
- **Context:** Run this BEFORE the price drop, and again right before it, so the prospect
  is doing ROI math — not price-shopping — when the number lands.
